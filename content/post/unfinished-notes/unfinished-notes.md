As I’ve penned the final article for this blog project, insecurities and negativities have shown forth in said article, which I wish to not be present in the final presentation. I’ve taken what I could from these thoughts and have cut off and left the rest to develop in the form of unfinished notes way down below, much in the same way that in the course of evolution, man has taken what he could from the animal archetypes, leaving what he couldn’t take into his own being to flourish as its own. As a result, the penultimate article here comprises the below notes by which one can derive *most* (the ones not dealt with implicitly interspersed in articles such as [*The Significance of Thinking*](https://goddesscameo.github.io/p/the-significance-of-thought-thinking-for-this-age-and-the-next/)) positions of departure from objective idealism into spiritual science. My goal after this blog project is to produce *productive* works, rather than focus solely on the dismantling or analytic dissection of another. Because of this, prior to the rough outline of the arguments you will find below, some clarifying points will be of service to the reader in order to close this chapter in discursive engagement.

In my engagements with objective idealists, a tendency has begun to sprout, typified by a corruption of the phrase “not I, but the Christ in me”, towards “not I, but the world-spirit thinking through me”. It is a revival of that all pervading imperialistic Spinozist substance, denying the freedom and moral culpability of the individual thinker. No such action is as cowardly as hiding behind a thought form, whether it be a holy book or some other authority such as an ideology, in order to abdicate oneself from the responsibility of being in the world. It allows what the person engaging in such actions, in this case thinking, thinks of as free reign in order to merely point out structural insecurities with the “objects” in question. From their perspective they are truly pointing out faults in the world, inadequacies with respect to the is-ought dynamic, inadequacies which ironically are truly vindications of the same world-historical process one would claim to defend. Surely, self-righteousness is the most pernicious sin in modern day. As a matter of fact, the disposition one has can be critiqued thusly: The main problem is on part of a *self-limitation* of the thinker. Certain people look for the absolute limits of what they can do with their own thinking, and as a result they overdose on Mercury, being completely plastic and moldable towards a particular system of thought. They become Mercurial men, possessing that same character of nature which logical conceptuality laments as the actualized potential of externality (the myriad forms and variations in nature), that which is impelled upon by a force they falsely recognize as their own and which limits their thinking capability. They are beings of quicksilver, unable to recognize their self-sabotaging. And so because the "rules" of their thinking are set in stone, critiques against the rules would be a contradiction in their supposed and perceived being. Such critiques are denied from the outset as external to the externality they have internalized. In effect they say to themselves, "because thinking *absolutely* has to be this kind of way, then *my* thinking has to be *this* kind of way, and I can accept no other way because I have falsely identified this thinking world-spirit as me". This is to say, conformity to an adopted system is mistaken for the freedom of self-determining thought when it is the problem baked into this psychic structure is the reification of a particularized conceptuality.

To steelman the Hegelist position, the interesting question is not whether Hegel would have agreed to this or that proposition or state of affairs. This is as daft as it is foolish. It is really the individualized revelation brought on by the thought form of logical conceptuality. Yet here is the clincher… The standards by which we are judging whether Ozempic is permitted, or what the Hegelian stance on the current culture war issue is is abstract in the truest sense of the word! One only needs to ask themselves *what* thought is and, more importantly, *if it changes* to have the whole organic schema move beyond Hegel. Because *if* thought changes, that is to say, *if philosophy has a beginning*, then *it has an end*. If, as the byproduct of a particular thinking, the system of philosophy, is conditioned by and determined by the quality of activity man exhibits, and this activity is subject to change and development and growth, then the realizations brought on by the Hegelian system of philosophy, for as much wisdom as is contained in it, will never fully be able to go beyond itself and grasp novelties in development. Look no further for proof of this than the way history has developed. An attitude was captured by Hegel's student Troxler in the following verdict. “Hegel has brought speculation to the highest stage of its perfection and in the very act of doing so he has destroyed it. His system has become for this intellectual current the last word; its indirect verdict is: Up to this point and not a step further!”, he claims, as the questioning of how his thought form may possibly attain to the standard of an inner life in itself, producing the world, rather than producing the content-form by which philosophical introspection builds itself on. Because of this *lack* on side of the world, proceding from Hegel’s death an unabashed explosion of materialism, empiricism, Darwinianism, and atheism, particularly in the form manifested by Feuerbach, took root in the world. Hegel’s *Philosophy of History* is a section of his work that needs the most improvement, but it is telling that even today, there is no explanation of the events after Hegel’s death and how precisely it advances the march Spirit. A kind of fanaticism then results from the totalizing correspondence theory of concept to object. Mirroring the crowning ethos of Islam in the age of warring tribes and "polytheism": There is one God and one God alone and his name is Allah, the concept. 

The fact of thinking being *in situ* with respect to man should not be overlooked. Connected with the aforementioned *lack* is the fact that Spirit is entirely abstract in the whole of Hegel’s system, precisely up until the point when it is actualized by man. The claim of divinity being logic is truly a kind of anthropomorphism, and so the irony of history shows itself in the fact that Feuerbach appeared when he did at the right time to answer this neglect of the world left by Hegel, or rather, the solipsism of the concept. Hegel’s “great chain of being” and ensuing angelology and theology is so bereft of inspiration that it naturally leads the world to produce an atheist such as Feuerbach. Avoiding a chance to shill my own writings is not in my character, so in order to expand on this “abstract” claim a bit more, previously I have written:
> The seed we begin with should be stated bluntly: Hegel’s cosmology was simply not convincing to me. The irony of this reason being unconvincing to the reader is not lost on me. However, it’s the truth! The more I mastered Hegel’s system, the more I gained an almost visceral dissatisfaction. Spirit for Hegel is abstractly lord. I will make two more points before posting the outline of notes. The first is on the fundamental distinction and difference between Hegel and any prospective system, namely anthroposophy. Here is the difference: The primacy of thought is in *thinking itself*. For Hegel it is in the concept. Read this over and truly appreciate the difference, since it is the difference between a Hegel and a Steiner, roughly in terms of presentation (which is the whole point, isn’t it? The way something presents is indicative of its idea!). Hegel makes the individual thinker an expression of an alleged objective conceptual process, obliterating individuality *in the general sense*. Steiner’s entire onto-epistemology is designed to establish how individual thinking can be both genuinely free and objective. For Hegel, the Concept is an objective, self-determining logical structure as the apical claim of *objective* idealism, compared to the previous critical philosophy or other species of subjective idealism. The concept is therefore the underlying structure through which actuality determines itself. What is done here is to give primacy and originality to the concept as an objectively significant expression of the intelligible structure of reality. For instance, an organism has its own determinations, relations, and development that belong to *its* conceptual structure. As a result, *the concept*, as in, the *concept of concepts* itself belongs to what reality is. For Steiner, there is a reversal of explanation as the true fulfilment of the world-historical process. As Henri Bergson says, “The universe is a machine for the making of Gods”. There is an independence and self-determining character of the activity through which concepts arise (thought), without automatically attributing that independence to the product of thought (what Hegel claims is the unity of thought with being, ultimately the form of the logical concept). Thinking in Steiner establishes itself as an independently identifiable activity with its own life.

The second point to be made is related to a performative contradiction. “How can you claim Hegel and the logical concept is inadequate when you are using it in your explanations?” Such species of claim are made only by someone who does not understand Hegel or reason. An equivocation is being made between using thinking and accepting the whole Hegelian schema. Acting as if reason in the general/generative sense automatically establishes Hegel’s ontology is begging the question. Because thinking has objective validity, Hegel’s particular explanation of that validity must be correct? This should immediately be rejected. Thinking directly participates in objective reality. Moreover, would the demand be that someone *avoid* logical formulation when making an argument or explaining something? How then would it be impossible to avoid a logical reconstruction or projection onto the object? It would be up to the good faith of the thinker. To not use logic in an argument, which by its very character falls under the auspice of reflection, would be moronic, just as it would be moronic to not use music in a song. 

Many avenues of explication and elaboration and criticism or commentary on the Hegelian age of thought may be had. At the end of the day, reason reaches out and parsimoniously adapts itself to enlightenment. As Hegel says, you can bring a horse to water, but you cannot force it to drink. The horses which have come here to drink may browse below the outlines and notes against the trend of thought known as Hegelism.

I have previously stated:
 > It’s a strange thing that Hegel’s teachings aren’t exactly new. His core idea, that the physical world, or object, is the externalization of what Steiner calls cosmic thought, as the solution to the chasm of subject and object left by Kant, is in essence, identical to the foundational doctrine of Western esotericism and occult secret societies: the world is created and sustained by thought- divine ideas, archetypes, and spiritual beings. Hegel didn’t arrive at this through occult initiation, but through rigorous philosophical reasoning. It did not matter, for example, that his contemporary *Fichte* was a Freemason, or other figures in his proximity such as Reinhold were the same. Through naively observing this truth in the structure of reality itself, and systematizing it in his logic, he grasped this doctrine. The content of his philosophy is esoteric wisdom, but his method was public, rational philosophy. Hegel himself comments on the esoteric/exoteric distinction in the Lectures on the History of Philosophy, stating that such a distinction does not exist in philosophy, since the only necessary thing is the necessity of the ideas. One cannot hold onto an entire idea as they do a physical item. Perhaps this is why there is a tragic poetic irony, or correlation, in both the fact that Hegel’s own works were published fairly late in his life, and the grand system contained in his works were destined to be understood only long after his time.   

-Supposedly, in philosophy, being and thought are one. But what does this truly mean? “Thought” in Logic is not our act of thinking, not even because intersubjective agreement on the process wouldn’t be enough. Suppose every person happened to have the same psychological architecture. That could establish a universal structure of human cognition, but it wouldn’t yet establish the *objectivity* of the categories in the system of philosophy (a bit redundant, philosophy proper is always a system. Of what? The reader may explore this further). Hegel’s claim is that the Logic is abstract/above empirical subject matter altogether. He is asking what are the determinations of intelligibility as such? As a consequence he is able to say that logic has traditionally treated the forms of thinking as subjective (with a few exceptions), whereas his task is to investigate them in their objective/absolute significance.   

Why then should the determinations of pure thought be determinations of being? It is not enough for Hegel to say “We all think in terms of Logic; Being, Essence, Concept, and so forth”. That would still leave Kant’s problem intact. Perhaps these are merely the conditions under which *we* may be able to experience or understand reality. Why should reality itself have that structure? The answer is because this formulation already presupposes two disjoined and yet complete objects brought together in the proposition: thinking and being/the world. Hegel effectively and absolutely undermines the independence of those two poles at the level of the Absolute. The determination Being, when thought through in its purity, exhibits an internal structure that leads beyond itself on its own standard, ergo the dialectic is immanent. For Kant, there is a strong tendency toward the form as supplied by the subject, and content as supplied by the object. Because of this, sensory content (subject) and subjective conceptual form (object) are brought into union to provide experience. However for Hegel, a true determination of thought is simultaneously a determination of its content. Logical categories aren’t mere empty containers, they are determinations of intelligibility that belong to the object itself. The object in question is “the whole”.   

There is a potential trap here, since in saying Hegel discovers the conceptual structure in the object, we run the risk of turning Hegel into a kind of metaphysical realist who believes that all objects contain little logical categories inside them. Because the “object” prior to conceptual determination isn’t something we can even have knowledge of, that is to say, because any object brought into consciousness is always-already *in thought*, objectivity itself is and has to be conceptually determined. And so the Concept isn’t a subjective representation of an object (psychologism).  

The logic gives us pure determinations of thought, and those determinations are themselves a content-form, weaved from the activity of man in order that it constructs a tapestry for him to gaze upon. But what warrants this “tapestry”’s (roughly used) relationship to or application with determinations of actual things? There are a few senses of “form” in the term “content-form” that can be distinguished which may be of use, but the correct usage will be elucidated accordingly. Take, firstly, the logical category of cause/effect. This is itself a *contentful* determination, saying something about what it means for something to be a cause, what an effect is, and the mandated relationship between them. Likewise with substance, universality, individuality, actuality, and so forth.   

The categories are *forms of determinacy* and so Hegel would not dare claim that the logical Idea contains empirical information about nature and the world. Logic establishes something like the conditions and structures of intelligibility of determinacy, not the inventor of empirical reality. This is why a philosophy of Nature is necessary at all! If Logic already gave us actual things, Nature would be redundant. Rather, the transition can be best described as Logic determining the Concept in it’s *self-sufficient universality*, to Nature as the Concept *existing in externality*. Empirical sciences subsequently determine the particular contents of nature. In other words, Logic comes structurally prior to determine the form of intelligible determinacy in order that this determinacy in external existence (Nature) be determined by the empirical sciences.  

Form as *our form of access* and Form as *the object’s determinacy* are different senses of form. Surely, if form meant the structure through which a finite subject can apprehend an otherwise inaccessible content, this would mean that form is epistemic and that content is ontologically prior. Hegel’s project would have been dead on arrival. However, because form refers to the determinate way in which something is what it is, there is no problem in saying that form is simultaneously logical and ontological. Because even objectivity itself is not structurally outside conceptual determination and actually arising from within it, according to Hegel, logical determinations are *absolutely* valid. The question of particularity, in the sense of an individuality that is, is the job of Nature. And this is precisely why the philosopher doesn’t deduce that there is this or that particular species from the Concept alone. It is only once there is a dog, for instance, that philosophy asks what the conceptual determination of its natural existence is.   

And so a sphere of conceptual intelligibility standing over and against an absolutely non-conceptual being is itself an incoherent abstraction.  

In the schooling of Hegel’s system, we are able to say that the perceptual object is not fully intelligible apart from its conceptual determination. Steiner says this exactly. Yet, he brings up an important question in which Hegel’s Logic is not equipped to ask or answer, let alone even consider. What happens if the capacity through which the world is disclosed develops? This is seriously something to consider. If Philosophy has a beginning, it has an end. If man was not always “thinking” in the manner he did now, as Julian Jaynes’ monumental book implies (as if the fruits of humanity through art, stories, language, religion, etc. wasn’t enough), then doesn’t this imply a telos or development? Setting aside the relative relativity for now, the opposition does not seem to be percept against concept either, with the logical Concept as the *final form* of intelligibility. Conceptual thinking itself can and will be transformed, with this much being granted as true within the domain of a logical consideration. Moreover, the object itself, in addition to man, will disclose further determination of its being as the activity of man becomes capable of perceiving these same determinations. It is a bit dizzying when trying to imagine this- a dual “movement” of man and the world occurs vindicating the insights of Heraclitus.  

And so when Steiner calls reason “the lowest form of clairvoyance”, for sure he is not claiming that ordinary reasoning is a kind of mystical vision. He means something more akin to the notion that thinking is already a mode of direct participation in intelligibility.  -There is no way to say Steiner is Hegel plus clairvoyance, although this should be separated from the idea that any system of spiritual science like Steiner’s would encompass and contain Hegel.  

The Idea for Hegel is already the unity in which Concept and Object are comprehended. Logical development would be mistaken to consider itself an incomplete version of reality waiting for clairvoyance to supplement it. For Steiner, at once he provides a developmental ontology of man and the ontology of intelligibility, whereas for Hegel only the latter is had  

-Hegel encounters the World-Spirit as logical Idea (and even supersensible feats, parapsychological capabilities for Hegel are the individual sundering themselves into it, rather than a capability developed by the individual. Not that true individuality is a thing with Hegel), then mistakes the Idea’s self-development for the actual self-development of Spirit. Spirit certainly expresses itself through ideas, and so the opposition is not spirit contra logic, but that Spirit being identified with the logical Idea itself. This is what Steiner means when he says Hegel sundered himself to the fetishism of logic. Of course a fetish in this context is something through which one was originally supposed to encounter something higher, but which becomes treated as though it were itself the higher reality.   

-Why does the persistent criticism of Hegel as a subjectivist arise? Because in truth, Hegel is a little *too* naive with his reporting. He sees logic and so thinks logic is all there must be. He is too willing to identify the spiritual reality with its manifestation in logical thought. Reality is more than logical self-disclosure. In other words, Hegel mistakes a medium of revelation for the being that reveals itself. One can say that someone’s speech genuinely expresses them, but this doesn’t mean that this person is nothing but their speech. The level beyond that, and the metaphor to the logical Idea is to imagine that you discover a deep internal structure of relations in the person’s speech and conclude that you have grasped the person completely.   

Conceptuality discloses Spirit, but why identify *that* disclosure with Spirit itself? Because we have no alternative? That’s like saying bad science should continue because there is no alternative.  

For Steiner the logical Idea is a manifestation/reflection of Spirit in human thinking instead of the totality of Spirit’s being. That’s precisely what Steiner says about Schelling and Fichte too! For Fichte it is a kind of reductionism to willing, and so the *whole* of man is reduced to the *idea of man* as generated by that will. For Schelling, he looks for Nature’s Spirit in the reflection of Spirit present in human thought (a la the pre-conceptual origin or whatever terminology he uses. Pre-unthinkable? Refresh yourself here). Finally for Hegel, who encounters Spirit through the Idea and confuses Spirit with the Idea of Spirit, it would be easy to say that Hegel got trapped in concepts and so we should abandon concepts and return to more immediate forms of perception. Instead, Steiner is saying that conceptuality itself can become an organ of perception, and thus the mistake is in absolutizing one mode of thinking’s disclosure of Spirit.  Suppose you find a tree. At one level you encounter the percept and proceed to the concept, progressing from immediate to self-mediative. But at a higher level, the same tree discloses itself through further determinations, perhaps stated as post-logical (retaining logical coherence but not being exhausted by it). Ordinary thinking is already direct participation with spiritual reality but not yet all of it; spirit is objectively present in thinking, and so when the Idea transitions into Nature, or in other words, when the abstract determination is freely released into concrete Nature, it may be said that Hegel has already determined in advance *what Spirit must become*, because he has made the logical Idea the measure of Spirit.  

-This “lowest” form of clairvoyance (reason) becomes indispensable even after supersensible cognition, just as the seed becomes indispensable for the tree. 

-Even if Hegel says Logic is the shadowy realm of the Idea, it is considered only full with the addition of Nature and Spirit (so still within his system). There is a reason people are compelled to reach for the spiritual world (or “true reality”) beyond his system. The trap is that Nature exceeding the Idea has already been considered by Hegel and so this *excess* is built into the system. In addition to that, Hegel’s Idea is objective *and actually spiritual*. However it reaches the “supersensible” as it is accessible in pure thought. He does not reach the supersensible as an independently experienced spiritual world. “Headbased” thinking, that is, body based thinking still occurs. It is an abstract super-sensible. Hegel has discovered the supersensible dimension of the sensible world, yet not a supersensible world in its own right. The world-spirit really does find itself again in thinking, and so the circle completes in a way. Hegel discovers that thinking itself is an event in which the universal essence of the “world” (classically this means the object, nature, cosmos, etc.) comes to itself. Because Hegel’s philosophy has demonstrated that consciousness can get beyond the merely sensory, because once you’re no longer a strict “materialist”, you can continue this line. Thought can rise above sense and naturally gives one reason to ask whether the spiritual itself can become an object of perception, rather than being the conceptual essentiality of what sense perception presents. Any further spiritual science cannot retreat from Hegel into irrational mysticism.
