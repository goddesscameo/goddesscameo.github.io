## Ontic Limitations

Modern cognition crowns reason and logos as it’s chief modus operandi. Conjure in mind the current world of cognition, of logical relations and the system resulting from this network. A world is rightfully identified as a system of relations, and so the current world of cognition we inhabit will be roughly identified as the world of logos; the world of relationality and self-reflexive deliberation. However, given the nuggets of wisdom provided such figures as Alfred North Whitehead, Jean Gebser, Heraclitus, Hegel, Nagarjuna, and Aztec metaphysics, in other words, the form of procedural generation, that of an inscribing of the self movement of being, it would be unwise to consider this modernity as static and eternal. Logos did not appear spontaneously. Emerging from a prior condition, just as adult emerges from child, or plant from seed, every developed faculty rests on a simpler faculty which makes the genesis of a new faculty possible in the first place. If we take our current cognition for granted, we overlook the fact that it had to be built up in some way or form. Thus, we may be permitted to conceive of a differing cognition that inches ever so slowly towards the kind we are familiar with. This priority is given to the faculty of memory. Before man could think abstractly, he needed to be able to hold the past steadily before him. Any application of a universal rule a la the logic principle must recognize a present situation which resembles a past one, that is to say, a line of self-continuity would need to be established, and prior to that, the establishment of continuity at the most basic level. This act of base level continuity in question is the faculty of memory. The capacity for stable, pictorial recollection acts as the previous interiority of an earlier phase of human development, present in weakened form even now in certain animals. It was out of this *ground* that logos would sprout from. Consider what logos demands. To operate, one must hold multiple elements in mind simultaneously, and to compare, one must retain one image (or concept for the modern bias, we will address this shortly) while inspecting and appraising another. To reason, you must remember that A preceded B, and so without a reliable faculty of memory, none of these operations are possible. The abstract thinking prized of modern man is in fact impossible without a stable foundation of concrete recollection. If memory is a prerequisite for logos, then memory had to have been developed *before* logos could arise. And if it was developed, then there was a development when memory was the most *novel*, the most advanced, or highly developed faculty that human cognition possessed. It was the cutting edge of humanity, because it *was* humanity’s function, just as we rightfully say that the current activity of man, *thinking*, is what characterizes him today. This period and system of relations is what can be called the world of memory. It is the cognitive epoch in which the retention of percepts was the supreme human achievement, prior to the emergence of logic. In that world, memory was not the tool serving thought, because there was no “thought” as we know it. Memory during this period *was* thought, as the highest faculty defining the entire horizon of what the human mind could do and be. What is asked is not to abandon logos in this ensuing conception, but to recognize that our current mode of cognition has a history, and that exploring this mode of cognition thoroughly will yield insights about our current. 

### The World of Memory

Now let us attempt to reconsitute this world of memory and draw out the implications. Consciousness would not present itself as it does today with a mental space, or mind’s eye, where one holds abstract disembodied concepts like “justice”, “number”, “freedom”, “causality”, and so forth. Rather, the mind is a continuous streaming gallery of sensory impressions. Every situation encountered is *re-cognized* rather than anaylzed. When looking at a particular arrangement of stones, one would not in their mind’s eye see things like weight or measure, one would instead have a picture of a similar arrangement of stones arise in mind, perhaps witnessed in youth, when your father placed them that way for a purpose. This *remembrance* carries the full sensory intensity of the original; the warmth of the sun, the texture of the stone, and so on in this memory constitute understanding. Nothing is added into it,one lets the stones speak for themselves. Reasoning, similarly, is reconstituted. What we call reasoning now would be transformed, or rather reverted, into the natural resonance of one vivid picture calling forth others. The reasoning we have now links concepts together through logical connectivity, but the reasoning in the world of memory links memories together with a kind of negativity brought by the selfsame movement of experience. When one faces a decision, there is no weighing of pros and cons. You simply let the present scene invoke a cascade of similar past scenes, and the most compelling picture, that is the one that feels most familiar and densely textured, governs your action. Time is gone; all that exists is an eternal now that is thick with the accumulated weight of everything you have ever experienced. Past is known, surely, but future is the mere difference of now, nothing but a new situation that in turn becomes part of this eternal now. What of the social substratum? What of the social order and organization? Because memory is the supreme faculty, authority rests solely with the old. The person who has lived many decades has a storehouse of pictures and sensory impressions so vast and so finely differentiated that no young person could ever hope to match it. Any situation that presents a threat to the social whole finds the youngest silent and the elders conferring. Keep in mind they do not *debate*, since this would call into question the status of things like the reason embodied in an individual, they instead confer by calling forth the pictures of every previous similar threat and every succesful remedy. The elder who can summon the most relevant and most powerful picture leads. Even education is subject to this crowning activity of man- when one is a child, they were not taught rules or laws or given multiplication tables or grammatical structure. Instead, a child would be shown life in its fullness by the teacher, an older relative or respective elder with the authority to pass on memories, performed the acts of planting, building, healing, etc. in front of you. They would not explain the principles, firstly because they couldn’t, and secondly because letting you *see* the act in it’s complete context, with all its attendant sounds, emotions, colors, warmths, and so on, would prove much more effective in internalizing it. Years later, when faced with a similar task, that entire lived scene returns to oneself intact. In a way, it is like downloading a scene, and re-enacting it. Finally, the structure of communal ownership we have now would make no sense in a world of memory. The concept of “ownership” does not make sense. Everyone is always-already embedded in a web of shared experiences, and so the grove of trees that provided shelter for your people is simply *there*, as a fixed point in the collective memory. 

To be aware of this world of memory as the pre-requisite to a sort of frontier beyond would not make much sense, at least, in terms of this world as beings in the world of memory, it is a full world. It is a complete and self-sufficiently integrated form of human existence. It is only from our current mode of consciousness, that of the self-reflective and abstract thinking kind, that we may even *reconstitute* it in the first place. To speak of the world of memory, it is prengant with the fruits of our current cognition today; structureud by subjects and objects, sequence, causality, and by the very logical relations that the world of memory supposedly lacks. This means that every description of the world of memory is a translation from a language we do not speak into a language we do. The very instant we say that “in that world, cognition is not a re-presentation, or an abstract hypostasis of self-generating conceptuality”, we are *generating it in mind* without living it. We are subjecting a non-logical state, or perhaps an unconsciously logical state, to logical form. We cannot avoid this. Because we are logical beings, our positive descriptions of the world of memory are necessarily metaphorical. However, our negative descriptions are precise, and so we can say it is *not* sequential, that in our world cognition moves from premise to conclusion, from thinking to the world. In the world of memory, these distinctions are absent, we can only say that *this* structure is absent and indicate towards a rudimentary structure currently present, but highly developed. We can say that it is *not* abstract, that our thinking metamorphoses through particularity to arrive at universality, that the world of memory acts only particular as a reified pseudo-universal. We may even say that it is *not* detached, that there is no gap between knower and known, because the *concept* of knower and the *concept* of known does not exist, only the immediate unity of knowledge- yet even this is a metaphor drawn from our experience of separation. The deeper implication of this exercise is that when we describe the world of memory, we are in effect describing our own current cognition by proxy, since by the very act of description, including  it’s structure and limitations, it reveals *what* we are by the activity we undertake. Consider this: when we say that the world of memory does not calculate, this statement itself is a kind of calculation as a judgement of comparison, or in other words, a logical operation. So our description of a non-logical state is itself logical. The content says “no logic”, but the form *is* logic. There is a recursive knot presenting itself. We cannot step outside our own cognition to describe a radically different cognition, because the very articulation of it is an act of our cognition. Any world, or system of relations, that we posit is, in its very formulation, an object of our current knowing. It cannot estape being filtered, shaped, and constituted by the very faculty we are attempting to examine. The most that can be afforded to knowledge of the world of memory is that we may gain a *glimpse* of what it is like since implicitly we have already gone through that development, seeing as logic is *grounded* by what may be called here memory. 

However, this can’t be said for any future cognition. Take these conclusions and apply them to our current mode of cognition and the frontier beyond *our* current mode. Just as the leap from memory to logic was a structural transformation, so too will there be a leap from logic to something beyond. However, if we take this principle seriously, we must admit that we can say the frontier is *not* logic, just as logic was *not* memory, and that the frontier will *sublate* logic, just as logic sublated memory, and that the frontier will reveal the *limitations* of logic, just as logic revealed the limitations of memory. But we *cannot* say what the frontier *is* using logical conception. Any attempt to do so would be a logical proposition of a post-logical state, which itself is invalid by the standards of the world of logos. It would be like a memory-based being trying to describe a logical syllogism using only remembered pictures, only pointing to the absence of pictures, but never generating the syllogism itself. The frontier then can be described as a structural inevitability, as a determined corollary of the fact that our current cognition is one stage in a process. Its only accessible quality is its *otherness* relative to us, and thus, only a negative standpoint can be had with respect to it. 

### Concrete Reality a la Atlantis

Assuming that the development of consciousness makes itself real at the same time as it’s conceptual development, we should expect to see this world of memory in the world and not let it collect dust as a logical inference or abstract philosophical construct. It should be discoverable as an actual phase of human experience, with its own characteristic forms of life and cognition. This is precisely what Rudolf Steiner, among others, describes. In his account of human evolution, this “world of memory” existed as a historical reality in a period he called the Atlantean epoch, during which memory was the most novel, and most developed faculty of human consciousness. Before logic and abstract reasoning, before that rule-bound thinking we now use colloquially, there existed a phase in which pictorial retention was the very substance of thought. Steiner’s descriptions match up exactly with what a logically reconstituted world of memory could provide. Man remembered, and called forth living pictures of the past. The words of Atlantean man carried force instead of meaning. Man’s *techne*, still being embedded in nature, was drawn from the formative forces they could still perceive. Additionally, physical nature and the environment is radically unlike our own during these times. Man’s physical constitution is described as watery, consisting of soft cartilage rather than hard bone, and covered by a thin, net-like skin. The head, in particular, was hydrocephalic in form, resembling the head of a human embryo, with a very high forehead. Speaking of the head, this idea that the brain excretes ideas like the liver excretes bile is relatively modern. Steiner reports that during these times, thinking was not a function of the solid brain. Instead, the Altantean could think with the water of his own bodily fluids. When this water element receded and the body hardened, the capacity for that form of thinking was lost, and the low-skulled forms (like the Neanderthal) appeared as a transitional phase before the modern high forehead grew back. Earthly conditions supported this constitution, as they always do (in the sense that there is a commensurability between an organism and it’s environment). The air enveloping the Earth was much denser than it is now, and the water over the entire earth was significantly more “fluid”, which was a crucial condition for the technology of the time. The geography of the Earth was entirely different as well. This should give an idea for the time frame of the Atlantean epoch. All of this corresponds precisely to what we would expect from a period in which memory was the cognitive frontier. 

The faculty of memory itself can be given a broad summation, however, Steiner actually details the novelties that different collectives of peoples during the Atlantean epoch develop. Meaning, surely, the faculty of what we call memory now is the *activity* of human being, but the history of the races he describes are the sequential stages of that activity’s development through time. Beginning with what he identifies as the Rmoahal race, what they mark is the *magic* of speech, of words that directly affect nature, then the Tlavatli, who bring about ambition and ancestor-worship, wher memory serves personal recognition. The individual shows signs of emergence, beginning to feel their own value, seeing as leaders are elected based on something individual. Then the Toltecs, who bring about commonwealths and hereditary dynasties- statecrafts built on shared memory. These are the first stable, harmonious societies. Education here transfmits ability from father to son. Next are the Primal Turanians, who innovate the use of black magic, or rather the *misuse* of the life-force sanctioned by the Rmoahals for selfish desires. Because the principle of individuation already began to show it’s hand, so too has the origin of egoism been birthed and the betrayal of spiritual secrets. The Primal semites followed, marking the beginning of logical thought and interiorization as the birth of abstraction and the loss of mastery over life-force. Penultimately are the Akkadians who mark the emergence of law and legislation, where external order replaces shared memory. The novelty in the world here is of restlessness, of enterprise, colonization, and commerce. Finally, the last race marking the overarching development of Atlantean consciousness are the Mongolians. The race group here represents a mystical conservatism that holds onto the ancient past while losing its living power. This (sub)race develops *thought-power*, but retains a nostalgic residue of the older memory faculty and the qualities of the fourth sub-race. Note: the modern populations using these terms do not correspond to the racial terms used. Now, the entire sequence is the slow and painstaking birth of the individual thinking ego, out of the oceanic picture-consciousness of nature. Whenever a new faculty develops, an old one must wane. The very power of memory, which allowed mastery over life, was destined to fade as the faculty of logical thinking began to emerge, and so memory, being tied to the past and to nature, lack the capacity to *restrain selfishness*. It could vividly recall personal power and achievements, which fed ambition. This is precisely why the higher faculty of thought had to emerge: to check the destructive misuse of life-force. By the time the fifth sub-race appears, logical thought has begun to eclipse memory, and with that loss comes the loss of command over the external forces of nature. The price of inner freedom, that is to say the ability to say “I”, is the loss of outer, magical participation in the world.  

Even now, we may look to the world of logos and track it’s development. The *thinking* of the Greeks, for instance, is nothing like the abstract thinking of the current year. During the times of Plato and Socrates, thinking rightly was *inseparable* from living rightly. Genuine knowledge of the Good was meant to transform the thinker into a better person. Modern rationality, by contrast, can be extraordinarily intelligent and logical while remaining morally neutral, and so a person can be both highly rational and deeply immoral. The reason for this is the evolution of consciousness currently happening, where *thinking*, this process of internal life generation, needs to exhaust it’s own functions fully and become *more and more* abstract, detached from the external world until it can arrive at goodness independently in the ego. Because the ego must learn to generate goodness from within itself and not inherit it from cosmic order or externality, it must withdrawal itself from the world, allowing the reality of the intelligently bad person. 

Now, this brings us back to the relative relativity of cognition. If every cognitive faculty is bound by its developmental stage, then how can we trust any description of a future or prior epoch? Steiner himself presents a very curious case we will return to later, related to this relativity. For now, it should be noted that for Steiner, the manner in which he describes Atlantis is more of a documented phase. It is, for him, not using the modern, abstract rationality we are familiar with. He claims to be providing a *positive* account for the future cognition in which *reason* is the lowest form of the kind of activity man is made to do (what he calls clairvoyance). The faculties he employs are latent in us but currently underdeveloped, and so his descriptions are perceptions made possible by a cognitive apparatus that has already begun to evolve in us, beyond the purely logical. Yet, when he speaks to us, he must translate those perceptions into our language. He must use concepts, sequences, categories, and metaphors that our current minds can grasp. Meaning, even Steiner’s descriptions to us are subject to the cognitive horizon. They are approximations, shaped by what we are capable of receiving. The good news is that this itself is very economical of *being*. In effect, it is being offering it’s own humility and inadequacy. Human being articulates its own finitude with respect to what is beyond it and subjects itself to the understanding of what is there. Surely, if reality were fully transparent to us at our current stage, *no development would occur*. We would have arrived and there would be no growth or freedom. The fact that we cannot fully describe the world of memory or the spiritual world is being’s way of witholding itself so that we might become worthy of it. That the current mode of cognition overdetermines all things and claims absolute knowledge is only testament to the fact that it is a form of evil; of finite being self-perpetuating itself without limit. 

## The Disappearance of the Event

### Heidegger’s Archimedean Point and Steiner’s Path

The *ontic limit* is the horizon of ordinary human cognition. Human being, in the current waking consciousness, is structurally attuned to entities in the form of their concepts, perceviging what is present, what acts, what causes, and what is caused. It organizes these perceptions (perceptions here does not only mean empirical items, it may include concepts themeselves) through logical categories, causal frameworks, propositional forms, and abstract logics as a required stage for the emergence of the independent ego and for the practical mastery of the sensory world by the very same. However, this itself represents a limitation of sorts. The fundamental question is whether human cognition can articulate realities that exceed its current state. The ordinary condition of consciousness, ordinary meaning current, is *bound* to the aforementioned organization above. And so to speak even of a grasping of consciousness itself, let alone the living Christ and Trinity, requires a movement beyond the conceptual inventory defining modern rationality and logos. Now, such a movement can’t possibly be an abandonment of logic unless one is committed to the idea that *thought does not change*, that thought has been like this since the beginning of humankind, throughout eternity. Such a position will not be entertained here. The human being is not trapped within its current cognitive horizon, in fact, they themselves can become conscious of their own limitations, and *this* self-consciousness is itself the first step toward surpassing the limitation. 

**Note:** It is through the wheel of dharma and through the successive process of reincarnation (as well as initiation for the chosen few) that this self-realization takes place. 

Heidegger can demonstrate for us that the modern understanding of truth is actually quite derivative. Truth, in the contemporary world, is understood as the correctness of a proposition in relation to a fact, of thought to being, or of concept to object. This can be called a generalized correspondence theory, that some *idea* assert itself and find itself in the world as a state of affairs. And so Heidegger locates the origin of this current ontic limitation in the transformation of the experience of truth. For the pre-Socratics, truth was known as *aletheia*, or unconcealment. It was the event of beings emerging into the open. Truth primarily was not a property of propositions or concepts and the world disclosed itself to the human being, and the human being dwelt within this disclosure. Subject and object were not yet rigidly separated. Plato initiated the split. By positioning the Forms as the truly real and the sensible world as a realm of appearance, Plato reoriented philosophy toward the correct apprehension of an intelligible realm, and the metaphor of *vision* became central. The philosophy turns his gaze toward the Forms and sees correctly. Truth then become the correctness of beholding. This transformation certainly was not an error. As explained prior, this was a step required for the emergence of the independent ego, but the risk that this change brings is in Truth *ceasing to be the event of disclosure*. Truth is now the correspondence of representation (or concept) to object. The subject stands now *over against* the object, measuring its assertions against the object’s perceived properties. This is the theory of truth that dominates modernity. Heidegger then claims that before we can assert that a proposition corresponds to some thing, the tree *must have already disclosed itself* as something that can be encountered. Correspondence of the kind mentioned presupposes the unconcealment of this being, but never actually asks about it. It takes the ontic relation for granted and forgets this ontological ground. Modern philosophy, science, logic, and so on operate entirely within this derivative framework. 

Heidegger is right about a few things. Firstly, something was lost of the pre-Socratics when Plato initiated the beginning of philosophy proper with the Platonic Idea. The idea of truth as *unveiling*, or *diclosure* can only reign prominent under two conditions. The first is if the ego which has something it is being unveiled in the first place is under less scrutiny, and subsequently development, as was the case during pre-Socratic Greece, and the second is if the ego has been sufficiently self-recognized and understood to the degree that the activity of man can now truly be focused on the world. Heidegger’s realization of truth as disclosure is perhaps one of his greatest contributions. He correctly sees that truth is a historical event that grants beings their appearance. He also sees that this disclosure is conditioned by our epochal and individual preparedness; that we receive truth in the manner we are fit to receive it. This is an immense recognition of the relativity, or subjectivity, of cognition. However, his conception of this disclosure remains fundamentally passive and receptive, framing the history of Being as a series of sendings. Being gives, we receive. The modern epoch is the epoch of *Gestell* for him, the enframing of technology, which reduces beings according to him to *standing-reserves*, and as a result, his response to this epoch is Gelassenheit, a furtherance of receptivity. Critiquing the active and willful imposition of categories onto reality yet not offering a positive method to actively prepare the organ of man for this next disclosure indicates only that Heidegger misses the developmentality and methodological requirement of the current epoch. Heidegger’s ontology, let alone his dialectic of technology, is much too one-sided. Following the transformation of *thinking* from the pre-Socratics to Plato and Aristotle is the gradual shedding away of old atavistic forms of consciousness to make way for the modern abstract thinking. Plato, still having not fully shed these old mystery school teachings was called a “half-seer” by Steiner for this reason. Aristotle, similarly, while being the beginning of philosophy as a *system-building* enterprise proper, still *saw* his 10 categories in the world as a living language, rather than showing how they emerge from abstract thinking logically. 

This pre-Socratic stage from which Plato and Aristotle are transitioning away from is described by Jean Gebser as the arhaic structure. We may take Heidegger’s concept fo throwness to establish the historical relativity of all standpoints, to reocnize that we are always already delivered over to a specific historical epoch, language, conceptual inherhitance, and so forth. We cannot evacuate this inheritance to obtain an external, absolute view. Every statement we make, or every question we pose emerges from a determinate, finite position. And so this relativity applies even to the “earliest” modes of human consciousness. The archaic structure, acting as the liminal placeholder, stands (in the context of the history of thought) at the threshold between a pre-human, undifferentiated continuum of pure being, into the first stirrings of human self-consciousness. This structure contains both a backward reference to the vanishing ground of total unity and a forward reference to the emergence of the “I”. Consequently, the pre-Socratic experience of aletheia that Heidegger retrieves is itself a relative standpoint. It is *already* a transitional formation. The early Greeks, inherited the pariticipatory modes of an older world, but they were already pressing toward the conceptual determination that would culminate in Plato and Aristotle. And so the forgetting of Being is therefore not a catastrophic fall from a paradise lost. It stands as the inherent movement of consciousness as it develops the independent ego, and defines the trajectory of consciousness. In other, more metaphorical, words, it is only *through* the fall that one can recognize there was a paradise at all. And so, there is a subjectivity to all standpoints, including the archaic. We cannot return to the origin. The only way out is through, recognizing that we are moving away from it and toward something else, always. 

Recently I’ve been enraptured by the miracles of Padre Pio. The stimata of Padre Pio represent a direct and irrefutable challenge to the ontic horizon of modern consciousness. These wounds appeared on his hands, feet, and side in 1918, persisting for 50 years, through blood and pain. After examination by multiple physicians who could find not natural cause, and concluding they weren’t the result of self-infliction, how else can one view this event? This event is ontic. It is both empirical and historical; a fact of the material world. However, the ontic horizon of modern science and philosophy *cannot* account for it. The stigmata are not a conceptual puzzle to be solved by subsuming them under existing categories. The closed system of material causality and conceptual determination cannot explain the reproduction of first-century wounds on a twentieth century body. Nor would the reduction of the stigmata to hysteria, fraud, or psychosomatic suggestion constitute an act of integrity. The wounds are definitely real, and their patterned character demands an explanation that exceeds the categories of philosophy. In many ways, miracles are *ontological scandals* for modern philosophy and for the ego, who would like nothing but to draw out all knowledge out of itself and it’s own understanding. However, what miracles reveal is that the horizon of ontic limitation is not exhaustive. It is as Hegel says, “the very fact that something is determined as a limitation implies that the limitation is already transcended.”

The negative gesture is the response of the logician and the philosopher. It would approach the stigmata with a pre-formed conceptual inventory containing categories for wounds, psychological states, historical anomalies, and theological illusions. The philosopher then compares the stigmata to this inventory and then forces the event into a pre-existing category, much like the Atlantean would engage with a new event through the world of memory. The stigmata then becomes a psychosomatic lession, or perhaps a “free expression of the Absolute Spirit’s return to itself”. In any case, this subsumption is an act of negation, it negates the event’s capacity to express itself as being. It negates the phenomenon’s right to exist *as* phenomenon. This negative gesture also has a practical and ethical consequence. It hardens the heart of the philosophers, immunizing the thinker against the real, and preventing any genuine transformation of the cognitive horizon.

Positive engagement reverses priority. Beginning with the phenomenon itself, the observer stands before the stigmata without a pre-given conceptual framework, stopping themselves from comparing the event to their bank of categories and pre-conceptions. In effect, the phenomenon is allowed to dictate the terms of its own intelligiblitiy, and when the phenomenon does not fit the existing concepts, the positive engagement discards the concepts and allows new ideas to be born from the event itself. Such a positive engagement would be truly generative, and in doing so would expand the cognitive capacity of the thinker, opening their heart to the possibility that the spiritual continues to act in the world. 

Steiner provides a positive way forward that Heidegger overlooks. In fact, his entire project is the systematic and practical cultivation of *higher* cognitive faculties. As transitional beings, living in constantly transitioning times, our being is a process, a becoming. Given what we are, we can say the truth of our current condition with honesty. We can say that we are historically situated, and we can say that we think with certain concepts. We can even say that we do not yet percieve the spiritual world directly (although this is not true, we all percieve dreams and have intuitions all the same). Yet, we are not condemned to remain int hsi condition. Because the “I” is precisely the organ of freedom, because personhood bestows upon man the centrality of idea, we have before us the capacity to transform ourselves. Limitation is real, but it is only developmental. Given what we are, we can speak *beyond* what we are. We can speak *of* the beyond, but we must do so in the manner appropriate to our transitional condition. Given what we are, we can say enough.
