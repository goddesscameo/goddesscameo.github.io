## Aristotle
![categories](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcSjaC4RfrWh74wK6w070vnCPqqKD-s8mLvbuXpcZxXD__zH8aCUA8PC1GBG&s=10)

Understanding Aristotle is the solution to many problems which make cognition cloudy and misconstrued today. All manner of total architechonic is present in his *Organon*, whether it be the sciences, spirituality, or a practical way to ground our thinking in the basic structures of reality itself. When we find ourselves lost in the sea of subjective opining and ungrounded assertions or skepticism, Aristotle’s work stands as a vestige from a time when the love of wisdom was prioritized, providing us an antidote of clarity and contemplation. Yet despite this utility, Aristotle is rarely given his due. In many modern curricula, he’s reduced to a footnote in the history of logic, remembered only for a dry linguistic formalism. In other circles, he is misinterpreted as a systematizer of mere subjective categories, a pioneer of taxonomy in thought, whose primary achievement was to create a filing system for our own mental projections. These miss the very real organic core of his body of work. Aristotle’s work in logic is describing the *real* insofar as they are the highest beings that are in the world, and so his primary aim was in answering the question of what *is*. It then makes sense that Aristotle’s Categories begin first and foremost with substance, because for him, the world is not a collection of events or floating properties, it is at the highest level a world of *things*, of self-subsisting entities. *Substance* is named as the primary reality, the being that underlies all others. And so a horse is a being, as is a man, and as is a tree. Their *qualities*, *quantities*, and *relations* are all predicated of the substance and cannot exist on their own. Beginning with substance, Aristotle establishes an objective anchor for thought by allowing it to discern the structure of what is. 

This structure is organic. As opposed to a mechanical view, which would see the world as a clockwork of interacting parts, where the whole is simply the sum of its pieces and change is an indifferent function of that interaction, Aristotle’s view posits a unity where the parts have meaning and function only in relation to the whole. This is what it means to act organically; to be organic is to be a self-subsisting whole that contains within itself an internal principle of movement and development, or a telos. Logos speaks to the intelligibility of this structure as the structure itself that makes a thing what it is, and what makes it knowable. Importantly, this implies a pervadingness of intelligibility or logic in both the subject and the world. In other words, what it says about both the organic system and the subject that there is a commensurability to them with respect to intelligibility and logos? It says that both we are intelligent, and that there is intelligence in the world, that we have the capability to be logical, and so does the world. Likewise, logic is not a tool we *impose* on the world, but rather the very language of the world as it self-articulates. Aristotle’s syllogism, for instance, is the formal expression of how the universal (the species, the form) is mediated through the particular to produce new, knowledge. It can be said that the deduction of syllogism is not in our minds, it is in the divine mind of spirit's self communion. To think logically is to think in harmony with the logos of reality itself. While some may see the classic syllogism: "All men are mortal; Socrates is a man; therefore, Socrates is mortal" as a tool for argumentation, it is in reality the logical structure by which mediated knowledge is self-articulated by the whole. This is precisely why thinkers like G.W.F. Hegel would make the syllogism the absolute form of all reality. In many ways, Hegel belongs in the tradition of Aristotle, providing defense against the claim of the categories being mere enumeration, and proving the absolute character of logic. The aim of this article is not to prove any of these things, however it is worth exploring for those not convinced of teleology, logic, and so forth and so on.

## Sephirot
![kabbalah](https://www.meisterdrucke.uk/kunstwerke/1260px/Athanasius_Kircher_-_Sephirot_Tree_of_Life_-_%28MeisterDrucke-980397%29.jpg)

Ultimately, Aristotle’s Categories represent the West’s attempt to articulate an ultimate reality. The correlative Eastern attempt (roughly) is found via the Kabbalistic Sephirot of the Jewish people. *Etz Chaim*, or the Tree of Life, is the name for this structure. It advances a system of ten divine emanations. Unlike a modern, who sees the human as a chance conglomeration of chemicals, ancient Jewish sages saw the human form as a kind of enacted scripture, a testament to the creative forces of the universe. They perceived that from the farthest reaches of the cosmos, ten distinct spiritual forces converge upon the human being, shaping him from without, and animating from within. These forces are creative powers, they are the very structure of reality made manifest. The “Tree” is thus a map of this interfacing between divinity and humanity, showing how practically the upper celestial worlds pour their influences down into the earthly realm, culminating in the creation and sustenance of mankind. 

The observant among you may have noticed a similarity already. Why ten in both Aristotle and Sephirot? The number carries both an esoteric and anthropological significance. Firstly, and of course most intuitively, it is the number of our own fingers. For the ancient peoples, the body was the first and most immediate instrument of the understanding. As Manly P Hall states in *The Secret Teachings of All Ages*,:

> THE oldest, the most profound, the most universal of all symbols is the human body. The Greeks, Persians, Egyptians, and Hindus considered a philosophical analysis of man's triune nature to be an indispensable part of ethical and religious training. The Mysteries of every nation taught that the laws, elements, and powers of the universe were epitomized in the human constitution; that everything which existed outside of man had its analogue within man. The universe, being immeasurable in its immensity and inconceivable in its profundity, was beyond mortal estimation. Even the gods themselves could comprehend but a part of the inaccessible glory which was their source. When temporarily permeated with divine enthusiasm, man may transcend for a brief moment the limitations of his own personality and behold in part that celestial effulgence in which all creation is bathed. But even in his periods of greatest illumination man is incapable of imprinting upon the substance of his rational soul a perfect image of the multiform expression of celestial activity.
Recognizing the futility of attempting to cope intellectually with that which transcends the comprehension of the rational faculties, the early philosophers turned their attention from the inconceivable Divinity to man himself, with in the narrow confines of whose nature they found manifested all the mysteries of the external spheres. As the natural outgrowth of this practice there was fabricated a secret theological system in which God was considered as the Grand Man and, conversely, man as the little god. Continuing this analogy, the universe was regarded as a man and, conversely, man as a miniature universe. The greater universe was termed the Macrocosm--the Great World or Body-- and the Divine Life or spiritual entity controlling its functions was called the Macroprosophus. Man's body, or the individual human universe, was termed the Microcosm, and the Divine Life or spiritual entity controlling its functions was called the
Microprosophus.

The human hand, with its ten digits, was a symbol of completeness and action, the means by which the human being grasps and interacts with the world. It is no coincidence that our base 10 number system arises, given this biological fact (I, for one, still prefer base-60). To the ancient mind, the cosmos in its full breadth was mirrored in the completeness of the human form, with ten representing the totality of a cycle or a system. The significance was not lost on the Greek esoteric tradition, which deeply influence Western philosophical and spiritual currents. Iamblichus and the Pythagoreans held the number ten (the Decad) in the highest esteem. For Pythagoras, the Tetraktys was the very symbol of cosmic order, encompassing the sum of all possibilities. It was considered the holiest of numbers, which were themselves akin to Aristotle’s categories, representing the universe itself, as a perfect all-encompassing whole. The number four signified the elements, the directions, the seasons, and their sum (1+ 2 + 3 + 4 = 10) ten signified the completed world.This Greek reverence for the decad surely resonates with the Kabbalistic structure, where the ten Sephirot are the complete emanation of the divine, and it goes without saying that the influence Pythagoras had on Aristotle is not to be discounted. Jewish numerology and mysticism similarly has 10 standing as a number representing the finality of union between the natural and supranatural: Ten Commandments, ten plagues, ten divine utterances, ten generations between Adam and Noah and Noah and Abraham, and Yud as the tenth letter in the Hebrew alphabet that links unity (Yehudi) with God (YHWH) and the particularized Jewish people (Yehudi), to name a few significances.

Now, the system of Sephirot is most distinctly represented in the form of Adam Kadmon, also known as the “Primordial Man”. For clarity, this is an *archetype* rather than a physical being, a kind of schematic of the spiritual structure of the universe as reflected in humanity. The ten Sephirot are distributed as the “body parts” of this perfect cosmic human, creating a symbolic mapping which shows the spiritual forces as *actual* and graduated forces in an organic hierarchy, moving from the most spiritual to the most earthly. Kether, or the crown, is the first Sephirah, the first emanation from the divine, an unfathomable “nothing” that contains all potentiality. It is the site of origin from which all remaining Sephirot unfold, corresponding to the “crown” of the cosmic human, the highest spiritual center. Chokmah (Wisdom) and Binah (Intelligence) are the eyes of Adam Kadmon. Chokmah is comparable to the intuitive flash of insight, versus Binah as the discerning principle of comprehension and organization. These two forces structure our perception of the cosmos. Chesed (Freedom) and Geburah (Severity/Strength) correspond to the right and left hands. The right hand (Chesed) is expansive, loving, and represents freedom and kindness, while the left (Geburah) is restrictive, powerful, and represents judgement and overcoming. These shape our action and experience. Tipereth (Beauty) is the central Sephirah, it is the torso, or heart of Adam Kadmon, the balance point of the higher and lower forces. That is to say, it is the principle of harmony and beauty that is the true center of the human being, despite what one may encounter. Netsah (Endurance) and Hod (Majesty) are the legs or thighs. Netsah is the force of movement and endurance, that inevitable overcoming might. Hod is the inner feeling, specifically the *splendor* of inner life. These represent the overcoming of the world in order to venture forth, and the reconnection and compassion to and with the world respectively. Next, there is Yesod (Foundation). This is the place of circumcision, the foundational and generative power. It is the force that grounds us, the sexual and creative impulse that is the physical foundation for manifesting life. Finally, there is Malkuth (Kingdom). This is the feet, the final and lowest Sephirah. It is the complete manifestation of all preceding forces in the physical world, the “field” or “kingdom” of our earthly activity. 

To merely enumerate these names is akin to possessing the alphabet without the ability to read. This is precisely the point made by thinkers like Raymond of Sabunde, The Sephirot become dry and appalling abstractions when divorced from their experiential verity. The *real* significance lies in how these forces are th be experienced and not in the mere remembering of the concepts. The student must practice this devotion so assiduously that the Idea begins to come alive within them, and then it can truly be said that one comprehends the Sephirot. Aristotle is often left to the wayside partly for this reason, the formalistic aspect of logic is grasped as representing the whole of the significance of the categories, and so the *Idea* which Aristotle envisioned does not come alive in the soul of the individual. Ancient Jewish sages certainly did not intend for their teachings as information to be downloaded and manipulated as Aristotle is now. The Sephirot for them were *spiritual letters*. Just as the physical alphabet is useless if one cannot combine the letters to form words and sentences that convey meaning, the Sephirot are useless if one cannot spontaneously jumble them together in their own mind to read the spiritual world. The goal was to learn a living alphabet where the letters corresponded to the spiritual forces in the world. A student back then would not just list Kether, Chkomah, Binah, and so on, they would combine them. For instance, to say “Kether Chesed Binah” was to proclaim a spiritual statement, that the highest spiritual power brings about intelligence through freedom, thereby designating higher beings who operate through this dynamic. This enough is true, that the task is to make these concepts roll around in the mind until they cease to be inert and become living realities. 

With this in mind, it is then that we may return to Aristotle’s Categories properly. Aristotle’s Categrories *are* the Sephirot. They are not two different systems that share a superficial similarity, they are *one and the same spiritual alphabet*, just articulated in two different cultural idioms for two different peoples. In the times of Alexander the Great, when Greek and Jewish cultures met, both the Sephirot and the Aristotelian Categories were understood as the same thing, as a spiritual alphabet for reading the cosmos. The Jews had their ten spiritual letters, and the Greeks had their ten: Substance, Quantity, Quality, Relation, Place, Time, Position, Having, Action, and Affection. Both are in effect “trees of life”, listings of ten fundamental terms. The Categories of Aristotle are the West’s Sephirot, and the Sephirot are the East’s Categories. Both are the two great, complementary alphabets of their spiritual world, both expressing the same structural principles of reality.

Now the understanding that these systems are one was lost due to a historical process of forgetting (slow down, Heideggerians) that stripped these living concepts of spiritual content. The process began with the transition from Greek to Roman culture. The Greeks, when they spoke of Alpha, Beta, Gamma, etc., did not think of such abstract, meaningless letters. Alpha meant the *spiritual* man, Beta, the *house* in which spirit dwells, and so forth. Every letter was at the same time a name and an image, carrying a world of meaning unto itself. Over time, this process of graded abstraction delinked the power of words from their usage, resulting in the fall of language into a matter of convention. This fate befell both the Sephirot and the Categories. The Jewish disciples, named the Chachamim, were meant to carry on the tradition, but forgot the art of reading the Sephirot. They continued to list the ten names, but they no longer knew how to spontaneously conjure them in the applicable order to form spiritual sentences and statements. By the Middle Ages, they were arguing over the 'correct' combination of words like Hod, Chesed, Binah, treating them as fixed formulas rather than dynamic, combinatory letters. Similarly in the West, Aristotle’s Categories cemented themselves into the dry concepts of school logic textbooks. Being, property, relationship, etc. became nothing more than abstract common nouns. The Categories ceased being the structure of the world that the mind needed to submerge itself in to comprehend. Enough has been said on this tragic, but necessary, process, and much more can be said on Aristotle and the influence he has had on the world. 

## Pairings 

### Substance and Kether
For Aristotle, Substance is the primary category, because it is the being that underlies all other modes, that being the independent self-subsistence upon which everything else is predicated. Kether is the first Sephirah, the “crown” from which all remaining Sephirot unfold. It is the highest primary spiritual power from which all else derives. Both stand as the foundational principle upon which the entire structure rests. To call the primary reality “Substance” is to say that being is self-standing; Substance is the Greek mode of naming the primacy of the real, and Kether is the Jewish. Both are declaring that there is a first, ultimate ground that is not grounded in anything else. The ground of being is the very *this*ness of reality, and so is *crowned* as that which rules all. 

### Quantity and Chokmah
Quantity concerns extension, number, and magnitude, the measurable dimensions of a thing. Chokmah is wisdom, the first outflow from the Kether, the principle of all life, and the expansive wisdom that serves as an outpouring that multiplies and extends itself into creation. The correlation here is found in the principle of extension, quantity measures the outward extension of substance in the world, just as wisdom is the first outward movement of the divine in creation. To call the principle of extension Quantity, is to say that reality has a measure, and that it is a cosmos ordered by number and magnitude. To call it *Wisdom* is to say that this extension is intelligent, it is in many respects revealing of the Category and vice versa. The cosmos has number because it has wisdom; it has wisdom because it has number. The measure of things is the self-outpouring of divinity.

### Quality and Binah
Quality determines what *kind* of a thing something is, that is to say, it’s essential character. Binah, as understanding, or intelligence, is the discerning that distinguishes one thing from another. Quality is what gives a thing its specific identity, Binah is the power that understands that specific identity by distinguishing it from what it is not. It’s worth nothing that the distinction is not as divorced as it is made out to be, particularly in man. It could be asked if a thing’s character exists independently of the thing’s understanding of it, but this is undercut by the fact that quality in man is itself in the fact of understanding. The knowability of the world is named Quality by the Greeks, and the elaboration that the Jewish concept brings is in the fact that this knowability knows itself by grasping itself. Ergo, the world has qualities precisely because it is knowable, and it’s knowability is in quality. 

### Relation and Chesed
Relation concerns itself with how one thing is with respect to another and to itself. Chesed is Mercy, Love, or Freedom as the expansive force which extends itself from itself to connect with the other. The very essence of relationality is in this movement, the self-gicing that establishes connections. To call the network of connections a relation is to say that reality is *interconnected*, and to call it Chesed is to say that this self-relation exists only by virtue of freedom, love, and mercy. It is a gift of self to itself. However this encounter may not always feel like a gift. No thing stands alone- to be is to be with, and the category of relation, or Chesed, is the rearticulation of a common substance that is held-together if not by love, then by some principle of cohesion.

### Place and Geburah
Place is where a thing is. Geburah is severity, strength, and judgement. It is the force that sets limits and distinctions. Place is precisely about boundary; a thing is *here* and not *there* in place. Geburah is the principle of limitation and containment as a force that defines and restructs. The cosmos is structured because it is bounded, and so place, as the result of limit, is the strength that gives form by proclaiming “thus far and no further”. They are one and the same.

### Time and Tiphereth
Time is the dimension of change and becoming. Tiphereth is Beauty. Beauty, in many classical senses, is harmony and proportion. Time, similarly, is the movement of things toward their fulfillment, to the degree that it tends towards a harmony of being. Tiphereth as the centrality of the higher and lower spiritual forces in the Kabbalistic tree is the *heart* of reconciliation. The cosmos *in* becoming *becomes* beautiful. Were Ahriman to have his way, he would prohibit this development, working from the past and freezing all into eternal repetition.

### Position and Netsah
Position concerns the arrangement of parts and the stance of a thing. Netsah is victory, endurance, eternity, etc. The latter is the force of overcoming. Position is the result of the force that enables a thing to maintain its stance against that which would displace it, and so it can be said that every stance requires an overcoming of sorts. Surely, to have a position is to have won it, and to have a stance is to endure in such and such way. The Jews saw this category as a power, as the victory of holding one’s ground against the forces that would de-position it. 

### Having and Hod
Having can also be called *condition* in the sense that this category is taken to mean it *has been conditioned*. For this reason, it can also be called *state*. Hod is majesty, an outer radiance of glory that conceals the inner reality. Having is, then being concealed, or conditioned, by virtue of something which occludes the inner reality. Outward manifestation is the connection here. What a thing has is what it shows forth. The Greeks saw this as a condition; a thing has shoes, has arms, has a state of being. The Jewish formulation of this being an outreaching veil in the form of radiant glory is just another way of resasserting the presence of divinity in creation, despite how it hides an inner secret, requiring us to interrogate it for secrets. To be equipped is to be glorious. The king is equipped with his crown, the warrior his sword and armor, and the sage his wisdom manifest. Possession then really is a sort of manifestation.

### Action and Yesod
To be equipped is to be able to act. Action is the active exercise of power upon something else. Yesod is the generative, grounding force that is the base upon which all action stands. All action is a kind of generation. At first, one might automatically pair Action with Netsah, seeing as Netsah sounds active, it is after all endurance. But if we follow the structure of the Kabbalisitc Tree, the higher determinations are gathered and transmitted downward toward manifestation. Action is similarly the category in which something *passes outward into an effect*. But it is only with Action that substance becomes agentic. It produces an effect *beyond* itself, and thus Yesod therefore corresponds to Action. All the prior Categories and Sephirot are gathered into an outgoing moment.

### Affection and Malkuth
The final listing here, the Category of *Being-Affected*, or Affection, is the counterpart to the Action of Yesod. This completion takes place in the field, or kingdom, or Malkuth, the final Sephirah, where all the preceding forces manifest, the site where the spiritual becomes most material. Malkuth is the point of embodiment and realization, where that which comes from above is received into concrete actuality. All prior embodiment stands for Malkuth as its representative. It is in the *kingdom*, the realm of matter that relates to the physical world, that all Sephirot pour themselves out into, and as it's correlate in Adam Kadmon shows through the *feet*, it is the most immediately accessible to earthly man. Likewise, it is less than easy for even the most uninquisitive minds to *see* the category of Being-Affected in the world. 

## The Four Causes and Four Worlds

![four-worlds](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/96/Jacobs_ladder_config.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=index&utm_content=original) ![four-causes](https://equivalentexchange.blog/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/sq3_four_causes.jpg)

Aristotle’s four causes present for us another avenue to explore when taken in consideration with it’s correlate. They are four different ways of explaining *why* something exists or happens. For Aristotle, a complete explanation often requires considering all four causes and not just the immediate physical cause. Listed forth they are as follows: the material cause which asks what something is made of (it’s matter), the formal cause which asks what its form is (its form), the efficient cause which asks what caused it, and the final cause which asks what it’s telos (final goal) is. We also have the *four worlds* of the Kabbalah, which describe four levels of reality, from the most spiritual to the more material. Firstly is the Atzilut, the world of Emanation. This is the highest level and the closest one to God, representing utterly divine presence. Next is Beriah, the world of creation. This is the realm of creation and intellect, where distinct spiritual beings and ideas begin to take form. Next is Yetzirah, the world of formation, where emotion and spiritual formation exists, associated with the shaping of spiritual forces, and finally there is Assiah, the world of action. This is the lowest level, representing the physical world and concrete action, the world we most readily experience. 

Because the four worlds of Kabbalah describe the graded levels of being, a *direct* structural correlate can be found in the four causes wherein Atzilut corresponds to Final Cause, Beriah to Formal Cause, Yetzirah to Efficient Cause, and Assiah to Material Cause. In the same way that the four worlds are the categories of spiritual realms, the four causes act as a field by which one can relate the different registers of being provided by Aristotle’s Categories. Now, the four worlds are registers that interpenetrate all others, just as each cause operates as a dimension of explanation that interpenetrates all others. Meaning Aztiluth is not only the respective world of Final Cause, but the *final cause register* of every Sephirah and every Category, just as Beriah is the formal cause register of every Sephirah and Category, and so on and so forth. Substance then is not “in Assiah”, but instead is manifesting *as* Substance in all four registers, as the material substance, as the formative substance, as the formal/archetpal substance, and the emanative substance. The same holds for every Category. 

## Conclusion
Internalizing the Categories as one does the Sephirot, and truly comprehending them, is important insofar as it sanctions the capacity for genuine and valid cognition. Without it, thinking remains prone to a false subjectivity, grounded by nothing real. The modern mind has been trained to treat concepts any which way in relation to a world that is ultimately unknowable or indifferent. By not submerging oneself in these concepts, one remains trapped in what can only be called a prison of sorts, ignorant to the reality of things.
