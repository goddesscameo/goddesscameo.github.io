## The Appearance of Evil

How is it the case that those with less material wealth are more eager to give than those who have more, and that those who believe in a judging God are less likely to volunteer themselves? Ordinary sense would align the ideals of a belief system with moral behavior, yet the poor give more proportionally than the rich do, and the non-religious serve more, regardless of motive, than the devout. The dichotomies and frameworks of cosmic justice and reward and punishment surely cannot comprehensively explain this. Something is amiss. And so to explain such a situation clearly, it is best to draw out the intuitive beginning from which a perceived anti-intuitive state of the world has developed. In the archaic condition, the unity of knowledge and action was immediately unified, ergo to know the good was to receive it and do it, and truth enacted itself seemingly without mediation. However, this immediate unity is indicative of a simplex inner life, much closer to the nature of an animal than to a free subject. For the good to be truly good, it must be consciously realized as mighty over all else, and as over and above all transitory and illusory states. Since this is the case, the site where this realization occurs must undergo some sort of processual event. Were it not for such a procedure, the good and true would not truly be thus on it’s own accord and instead be bereft in the subject, constantly offloaded onto another determinacy. For this reason, it is not right to say that the lion which feasts on the antelope is *evil*, since it does not have the capacity to choose good. The lion merely operates ultimately as a function of some other determinacy; it operates under instinct, under the watchful eye of a higher ideality it cannot participate in and with. Animals are, as their etymology suggests, merely *animated* forms. Surely, animals in the general sense have a sentience, but they do not have the ability to reflect on the actions they undergo. From the more basic hylomorphs in the animal kingdom slowly emerges the figure of “man” who *does* have this ability. Similarly, one can see, starting from the other end of the spectrum, that the highest angels of the godhead are not “good” in any conscious sense, since proximity towards the godhead has as it’s prerequisite the ends of a divine plan. In other words, self-determination is offloaded onto another determinacy once again. Slowly as one descends down the golden chain of being towards man, he is fixed in place as the category of being which functions as the whole of being’s self-articulation. The moniker of man is thus placed upon any being which is capable of and in the process of self-determination.

As a consequence of this unique positionality, man undergoes an evolution of sorts from the more archaic condition of receiving impetus from the outside in, towards the development of an inner life that can hold, manipulate, interpret, and integrate these outside forces, and eventually become the origin of action itself. This process makes itself real through the advancement of what we call time, and so we can trace this development anthropologically and see that the history of man is the history of an emerging self-awareness and inner life. We may even look to the biblical story of Adam and Eve in the garden and the ensuing fall from paradise as the story of man’s self-consciousness, as the beginning of the generation of inner life known as thought. Regardless, man develops in such a way. It is often said that “man is the religion of the Gods”. The Gods, angels, and principalities all pour their being and ideals into humanity and see as their highest realization the actual practice of our lives. They want to see the integration of what they give us transformed into concrete human action. Then again, we also receive from below. Beings lower on the great chain of being (such as natural spirits) sacrifice their substance in order that we be in the very first place. We stand between and must take what we are given and integrate this. Once again, we return to the processual event, the generation of inner life known as thought. [Thought is the generation of inner life](https://goddesscameo.github.io/p/the-significance-of-thought-thinking-for-this-age-and-the-next/). Throught thought, the external becomes internal, and the internal becomes externalized.

The emergence of thought in man is the origin of diversity for the very fact that thought is an internally generative act that allows one to draw out realities as an active reconstruction of the concepts that structure one’s reality. Likewise, the more contemporary idea of diversity rife with naturalistic and Darwinian assumptions, is explained away by the origin of thought. Insofar as migration patterns occur, thought becomes the tool capable of allowing the kind of adaptation we see in man. It becomes, prior to abstract thought which has no connection to the empirical world, the tool capable of starting with a group’s localized and conditioned inner life, and results in what some may call transcendental apperception in order that they perceive the concepts structuring the reality around them. Still, each individual’s thinking is localized and conditioned by *their* specific inner configuration, and thus thought generates diversity by creating multiple valid but partial views and modes of reality. Each one-sided, each revealing and distorting, and each finding it’s completion in the whole. All in all to say that the origin of diversity is the origin of inner life and self-consciousness itself. As thought develops and inner life generates, subjects differentiate. Each subject has a one-sided inner life. 

Inner life develops differently across individuals. Shaped by the times, by culture, by socio-historic contingencies, even by trauma collective and individual, what is clear is that this development is not uniform because the process of becoming self-determining is a continuous negotiation of and with the self and the outside forces of external determination. Each subject realizes the good through their own specific configuration. The buck ultimately has to stop at the individual. Because the subject only sees through their own inner configuration, they miss aspects that other configurations see. A given configuration filters, emphasizes, subordinates, and even reconfigures what enters. Additionally, the subject sees aspects that other configurations cannot see. The particular formation of a subject allows and restricts access to aspects of good and evil that remain hidden to others. The subject, as one which is in the process of attaining truth from within, sees wrongly and sees truly at the same time, because they see partially. What is true is true, and what is false is false, both arise from the same condition. Perception and concept formation, so mediated by the inner life of the subject, then cannot be given to us as finished objects. Man has to individually realize the true and the good by recognizing it and appropriating it as his own through his internal processing. 

On account of this overview, this provides us a return to the initial opening rhetorical. Because the good is realized individually, so too is evil. Two people may witness the same event and name its evil differently, creating the appearance that evil is a relativity depending on the individual subject. As opposed to the simplistic inner life of archaic man who dogmatically received instruction from without, the complex inner life of man has led to the mediated unity of knowledge and action, of thinking and being, and can serve to explain the apparent disparity between one who preaches one thing, and practices another. If nothing else, what is demonstrated in a given hypocrisy is the structure of mediation, this is true. Yet truth remains true, and the good remains good, and the relation between truth and action now streams through an inner life. 

Then what may be said about evil, is that the differential appearance of it does not negate its reality. Evil is *real*, and retains a status grounded in being itself. 

## All Things Are By Virtue of the Father: Evil Roughly Defined

Ignoring subjectivism, two options present themselves to us. The first is that evil is a substance, that it is a positive being with it’s own independent existence opposing the good. The second, is that evil is *not* a substance, and that it has some other mode of reality. Perhaps it is relational or derivative by nature. The first is difficult to sustain. If evil *is* a substance, it would be a positive being, and such a positive being would have to be something that exists in itself, with its own character, and own principle of operation. Good, having the same implications, stands as a second half and thus leads us to a dualism of two ultimate principles. One good, one evil, both in eternal opposition. Traditional accounts of the good would then have to be afforded to evil. If evil is a substance, then it must have some origin. How did this come to be? How did evil come to exist alongside the good as an uncreated, and why then could goodness not overcome it? Certainly if it is created, it must have been created by the good, which then raises the question of why the Good would create a substance opposed to itself, and how this functionally would be opposed to goodness. Additionally, for good and evil, if they are both substances, to co-exist, there would need to be a commensurability between the two, a higher unity which allows both to *be* in the first place. And finally, if evil is a substance, then it’s purpose would have a relation to the good. Substances are not a pure chaos. They have identity and consistency. This suggests to us that evil is then not an absolute opposition, but rather a subspecies of the good. The first options leads us to the second upon a basic inquiry. Evil cannot be a self-subsistence and so must be something else *in relation* to the good. 
Classic Christian theology supplies us with an articulation most fully, namely via Augustine. Evil accordingly, is a *privatio boni*, it is the privation of good. And this is more or less correct, as far as preserving the ontological primacy of the good, and accounting for evil’s parasitic character goes. However, this brief summary does not accurately surmise the positive affectation evil inflicts upon the world. As Rudolf Steiner said in a lecture on Jan 1914, *For anyone capable of thinking impartially and without prejudice, this answer—that evil is merely negative—stands on the same ground as the answer someone might give who were to say: “What, then, is cold? Cold is only something negative, namely the absence of warmth. Therefore, one cannot speak of it as something positive.” But if, when it is cold, one does not put on a fur coat or a winter coat, then one will already perceive this negative as something very positive!* Evil has it’s own dynamics, regardless (or rather, by virtue) of the source, and so in each case the good is present in some sense, it is never wholly absent. 

**Evil is good out of place.** When the good is detached from it’s *telos*, such as when order is detached from justice becoming bureaucratic and managerial, or when truth is detached from humility preventing the very communication of that truth, and finally when holiness is detached from love turning into idolatry, the good does not cease to *be*, it continues to exist in a mode that contradicts its own nature and functions as evil. It is in the process of passing away, but it still is. Just as the tyrant may still genuinely value order, and the fanatic foliness, the problem of evil is good becoming detached from the purpose that originally made it good in the first place. Historically we can take an example of “evil” and notice how this definition rings true: Attila the Hun, the "Scourge of God", was a spiritually initiated shaman acting out of Tao, or divine consciousness. The princes he deposed lived in luxury all around his kingdom, falling from the monotheism established as normative. But he, as a shaman, lived simply. Placing him in juxtaposition to those he would go after serves to lay the groundwork for the actions he took. They said of him that his eyes glowed and the earth trembled when he raised his sword. Truly, in previous eras, he would be viewed as justified; in our time, he would look like a criminal. The same power that is an expression of the divine fire in its time appears as divine wrath in another period. What once served development becomes regressive when its time has passed. It may be broadly stated that displacement is the mechanism of evil. This is how evil can be real without being a substance in itself. Displacement produces very real effects: it produces suffering, fragmentation, cruelty, destruction, and so on. These effects are *not* illusory in any way.

It would be unheard of for traditional theodicies to comprehensively explain much beyond the mere fact of evil in the world without an adequate account of this kind. This is to say, privation theory is ontologically correct, but such a theory cannot distinguish between absence and a *positive misrelation*. It treats evil as *nothing* and thereforce produces an attitude that fights nothing with everything, becoming a form of evil unto itself. What is produced is an attitude of conquest that flattens asymmetry, seeing all otherness as a form of resistance to the inevitable good. Ironically, this attitude is *itself* a form of displacement. The good of order when detached from love and humility devolves into the drive to dominate and eliminate perceived error. Individuals so intoxicated off their own righteousness undergo a form of ego death and forget themeslves as individuals capable of love, dissolving their individuality into a messenger for their perceived good. 

## King Solomon’s Lesson

Mediation is a structural condition of man. Seeing as the subject does not receive truth directly, it receives it through inner life, a particular spiritual disposition, tradition, and so on, in order for it to attain *some* recognition of the whole. However, this same structure of being is what makes displacement possible in the first place. Because the good is mediated through one-sidedness in finite life, it can be mesmerized through that particularity and seduce the individual. Seduction of this kind plays out in many different ways. Similarly, the way evil is experienced, named, and represented institutionally changes historically, from individual to individual, sometimes very drastically. Different cultures may speak of demons, sin, alienation, domination, or self-contradiction. These are all *historically* differentiated expressions that are nonetheless expressions of a real structured possibility: insofar as being are capable of realizing goodness, they are also capable of becoming divided against that very same goodness. 

The figure of King Solomon provides a poignant example by which we can explore this. King Solomon was a famous king of ancient Israel, known for his great wisdom, wealth, and building projects, including the First Temple in Jerusalem. He was said to possess a magical seal, often called Solomon’s Seal, which gave him power over demons. In the Testament of Solomon, he uses the seal to summon and subdue demons, forcing them to help build the Temple. The story later takes a darker turn and finds Solomon having become corrupted by his power and by his foreign wives and their gods.  In some later traditions, he loses control of the demons or the divine protection behind his authority, and his kingdom eventually falls into decline, although this is not strictly biblical. Solomon’s fall, in a very crude sense, represents the displacement mentioned prior. His fault was in managing competing interests while lacking focus and inverting the telos of himself and other. 

To begin with, his authority was never something he possessed, as if it were a magical substance he owned. One does not own divine authority like they do an object, just as one does not own truth. The authority that Solomon possessed consisted of engaging with and expressing a higher order, making this authority conditional. A human being can genuinely instantiate justice without becoming identical with justice itself, and therefore a genuine authority does not imply permanent inccorruptibility. The possibility of corruption only remains because the finite subject never becomes the self-grounding source of authority it exercises, it is only by virtue of this distinction that the subject is at all. Within this problem is Solomon's eventual encounter with Asmodeus and his divided allegiances acquire their true meaning. As the paradigmatic wise king, he was mistaken in acting as if wisdom automatically acts as a will towards the Good. One may understand multiple orders, and even understand the forces that threaten order, yet this does not ensure a continued engagement with the principle that makes one’s own authority legitimate. His capacity for integration increasingly became a capacity for managing competing principles, especially shown through the symbolism of foreign alliances and wives. 

Such foreign alliances, wealth, political arrangements, and religions certainly should not be reduced to isolated sins either. Together they represent a multiplication of centers of allegiance. Erotic attachment, mammonic accumulation, political alliegiance, and imperial administration like as differentiated spheres ceased to be mediated within a coherent hierarchy of ends. The many became independently authoritative and began competing for centrality, overall threatening structural integrity. Historical differentiation may become dangerous, however by itself it is not *evil*. Differentiation is not itself corrupt, diversity is a consequence of freedom in many senses of the world. The problem emerges when mediation becomes a self-grounding thing and when particular historical forms cease to recognize any principle beyond themselves as ordering their relation. Therefore, Solomon does not simply succumb to corruption, as though an external substance entered him and changed him from the outside in. **Corruption occurs through the reorganization of the relation between the whole and its moments**. Initially exercising authority because of his engagement with a higher order, his wisdom allowed him to incorporate and mediate increasingly diverse powers. Those powers became progressively independent and the principle that originally ordered them becomes one principle among others. His authority thus became displaced from the ground that made it authority in the first place. 

Parts from the whole, once displaced, begin to claim a kind of autonomy that contradicts the order through which they originally had their place. Demons are important in this story since they represent a fairly vivid form of differentiated negative power. Whether one interprets them literally or metaphorically, each demon has a name, a domain, a function, and so forth for a reason. Evil does not appear as one undifferentiated blob called “evilness”, it differentiates itself, appearing as lust, pride, illness, violence, deception, domination, false enlightenment, fragmentation, etc. It can be said that the differential appearance of evil does not negate its reality and is in actuality precisely how its reality becomes determinate. 

Initially binding the demons, it is worth noting that Solomon does not annihilate them. His authority consists in ordering and subordinating them, suggesting that the negative cannot simply be eliminated from finite reality, it must be mediated, or transformed, in some way. As such, desire, power, difference, and multiplicity, even destructive potential, cannot merely be wished away, they must be given their proper place within a given order. Thus, Solomon’s authority over demons is analogous to any higher being capable of recognizing negativity and *recognizing it’s place*. Solomon’s fall is thus tragic because what he formerly subordinated in many ways became a condition of his own existence and action. It is functionally the revelation that finite authority can become dependent upon the very powers it formerly believed itself capable of mastering. The collapse of one form authority reveals the persistence of another, and so Asmodeus’s victory is read as a force revealing that Solomon’s command over it was *never* absolute or self-grounding. In other words, the objective consequences of contradiction reassert themselves at the end of the day. The demons were genuinely subordinate, but only within a particular structure of mediation. When Solomon himself breaks that structure, the relation can reverse. 

The preceding reversal can be extrapolated into today’s understanding. Because the finite subject which aligns themselves with good sees falsity as lower than them, a relation of dependence can develop among those powers once subordinated. Another possibility immediately emerges in the form of the subject using the very truth that once grounded their authority as an instrument of its opposite. Before us is a very sophisticated form of evil beyond mere simple ignorance or error. The ignorant person may fail to recognize the truth, however the corrupt person may recognize something of the truth and nevertheless subordinate that recognition to another end. Truth is barred from determining action, and instead action determines in advance what the truth will be permitted to mean. The difficulty this presents is firstly in the gray area of truth as remaining present. Indeed, it must remain present if it is to be instrumentalized at all. The individual continues to speak of justice, the good, law, freedom, or virtue. They may even correctly articulate themselves. But these determinations no longer exercise authority over him. Their content is progressively subordinated to any genera of externality- power, self-preservation, ego, wealth, desire, etc. It presents secondly in the fact that falsehood no longer needs to appear as falsehood. 

There is a tendency to imagine evil as that which openly rejects the good. But this is one of evil’s least flattering forms. A developed form of evil may not necessarily say “Justice is meaningless”, it instead opts for “Justice requires that I do this”. It does not *necessarily* reject law, morality, and so on, but it may invoke them constantly. These principles themselves have not undergone any change, only a change in their relation to the subject has occurred and so what once stood above the subject as a principle capable of judging and limiting *them* is transformed into something the subject themselves administers and paradoxically hides behind. The truth remains syntactically intact while it’s authority has been widowed from enaction. The misuse of truth cannot be understood as lying for this reason, the liar at the very least preserves a distinction between what is true and what is being falsely represented. The subject reorganizes the relation so that the truth itself becomes instrumental to whatever idol is in mind. 

Every truth must appear through some determinate form, either a person, an institution, a religious practice, a tradition, a political order, and so on, yet none of these finite forms is identical with the truth it mediates. The distinction is what makes mediation possible, but is also what makes corruption possible. In the same way that a letter can carry a message from another person without being that person, a law can mediate justice without being identical to justice, and a church can mediate religious truth without being identical to truth. A person similarly may say true things here and there at some point, like poor King Solomon, but once this distinction is forgotten, and love is abandoned, the finite mediator can begin to falsely identify themselves with the truth. Ultimately, the lesson to be learned is humility. There is a fine line between upholding normativity (the fact that there *is* a way things should be) and getting so high off one's own perceived righteousness that it veers into evil and falsity. The full expression of truth needs to be defended with as much righteousness as the very fact of truth. It is not as if one thinks themselves doing evil either, in fact it’s quite noble to think yourself a defender of some inalienable truth. It is understandable why it’s intoxicating to act in this way. Nevertheless, a thorough understanding of evil’s relation to the subject is mandated.
