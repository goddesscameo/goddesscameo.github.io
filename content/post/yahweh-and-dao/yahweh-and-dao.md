A recognizable retort among enemies of what is known as Christianity presents itself as such: Yahweh seems to have found emergence within the Canaanite religious world and acquired traits of storm gods such as Baal; a cloud riding, thundering deity at war with cosmic forces presents itself as the likely origin of the Christian conception of God. If Yahweh’s identity developed through this process of religious borrowing and evolution, then the Christian God concept may be better explained as a contingent human construction rather than divine revelation, detractors argue. Very clearly, this does not follow. In fact it hides a premise almost laughable at face value- that the revelation of universal truth must present itself in an air tight form unable to be indexed by spatiotemporality, yet paradoxically allowing anyone (or rather, the enlightened modern) to recognize it as true. How this revelation comes about untainted and unfit for the recipient is a great mystery. The truth is that universality, that which conceived of as *total*, must necessarily impart itself in the form of a particularity at first. It must develop itself as a seedling from a constricted and limited locale, until it grows and flourishes, and the universal latent within it becomes recognized.

What such objections fail to see, and what the relationship of a universal truth to its particular emergence, and of course to its eventual individual reintegration, is that these domains constitute a single self-differentiating unity, rather than existing as separately connected. As such, the “total”, or the universal, is not an abstract common feature hovering above its instances indifferent to them, it is *already existent* as a concrete self-particularization. Contrary to the common notion of “universality”, where that which endures across change is raised up as a chief principle, prohibiting unessentialities from destroying the structural integrity of the total, the true notion of universality finds itself at home with its parts, since its parts are generated from it. As such, the universality of God, that divine truth, stands as a whole in free self-identity, containing the whole of its own determinations, in this case the actual manifestations of revelation. Particularity then rears its head as the specific historical form in which the universal is first posited. As relevant for us, this would be the Canaanite storm-god imagery, Israelite covenantal traditions, prophetic critique, temple cult, and so forth and so on. In the most literal of senses, particularization is the cleaving of parts from the whole wherein the universal is immediately self-determined concretely as that which specifies itself, finding itself acquainted with its opposite. 

The aforementioned retort fails precisely because it claims that if Yahweh emerged from a particular origin, then the Christian God is henceforth a contingent human construction. Such a standard assumes an *abstract universal*, a universal so abstract that any historical particularity would immediately contaminate it. This is a universal being so pure, it is in effect an indeterminate nothing. The reality of the situation is that the *real* universal is *concrete*. It self-particularizes and in the moment of individuality recognizes that the particularity of its origin is not at all a defect, but a mediation of this very same universality. In other words, the universal becomes recognizable only through its development in and through a particularity. The fact of Yahweh having storm-God traits no more disproves the universality of the Christian God concept than the fact that a seed has soil disproves the universality of the plant. Surely the soil is not the plant, but to have a plant grow and develop without the fertile soil which facilitates its growth is to have a standard so divorced from reality that it deserves nothing more than to be left to its own devices. Moreover, the detractor’s genetic account ignores what the name itself discloses. *Yahweh* is bound to the Hebrew verb *to be*, and in Exodus 3:14 it is given with the gloss *ehyeh asher ehyeh* - “I AM WHO I AM”. The given name is clearly, on *it’s own account* not a label for a nature deity among other nature deities, for a war god of a specific tribe, and so on. It is clearly a self-designation of being. Nigh, it is the name for the human “I” itself. However, the purpose of this article is not to give a lecture on the particularity of this revelation of God.

Another case study sourced from anthroposophical readings presents us with *Tao*. Rudolf Steiner argues that in Atlantean times, long before the current era, Tao was not a philosophical concept or a name for the ultimate totality at first. It was an *actual* formative force. It is with the power of this formative force - Tao - that the Atlantean could sense the rhythm of the upper and the lower, that he could feel and control the growth of plants, and so forth and so on. It was, in effect, the blood, air, the other. It *was* the particular name for an experienced total reality. As Steiner states: "In the Tao, human beings do not yet feel separated from God, either inwardly or outwardly”. Only later, through the emergence of an intellect working within, did the feeling of separation arise. Only *then* did the Tao become the universalizing term for the divine presence. It was through an abstract *sundering* and cleaving from man that *the way* became recognized as the ground of all things. Perhaps in the language of the logical Concept, we may say that the Atlantean experience of Tao was the universal in its immediacy. Etymologically, a point can be briefly made. Tao is written out as: 辶/辵 and 首 (“head”), so its concrete root sense is a road, a path, or the very act of leading along a *way*. The earliest uses are of a practical and immediate kind. Only later does it acquire the abstract sense of method and doctrine. 

In order to make the point of this brief entry most explicit, it is best phrased as such. A truth meant for all people *must* come in a particular form, spoken in a specific language, use a culture’s idiom and images, and appear at specific time and place to be determined by providence. That particular form is what is mandated such that its recipient actually receives the message. As for the revelation itself, its universal meaning becomes clear only after it has developed, and as such, the historical origins of a revelation ground this being in the world.
