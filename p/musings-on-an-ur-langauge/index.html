Musings on an Ur-langauge

The Possibility of Speech and Language

27 minute read

Intro

Language is proposed as the key signifier of the anthropos principle by such figures as von Humboldt, Cassirer, Aristotle, Herder, and so forth. Yet for the degree to which it is intimately and passionately employed, a treatment of language as such eludes all but the initiated. Neither is it in the grasp of the lover of wisdom nor in view of the layman who enacts it unconsciously as the mode by which self-reflection articulates itself today. In order for an accurate layout of language to present itself to us, let alone grasp a universal and generative form of it implied by the title of this article, it will be of use to look towards speech as way of orientation towards the concept of language. Speech is a mode of expression linked directly to the conveyance of the soul. Language as sign may be assisted by such things as sign language, gestures, and non-verbal channels such as gaze, facial expression, body “language”, touch, and any number of sense impressions we do not have the time to enumerate today. These non-verbal dimensions play a characteristic role in language, but the mere conveyance of meaning is not what is meant by speech. Even animals convey meaning. A bird’s song, and a whale’s call are expressions of inner states; these are expressions of the soul quality in beings and so even animals have the capacity for the conveyance of the soul. But human speech differs in kind. The ability to verbalize one’s experiences, feelings, and thoughts, that is to say, one’s inner life, is reserved chiefly for man. You only find rudimentary and fragmented forms of this capability in the animal kingdom. A dog may signal fear, but it cannot communicate “I”, much less “I was afraid yesterday”. It cannot refer to itself as a subject, place its experience in a temporal frame, nor can it make its feelings the object of reflection. Human thinking as the capability to articulate language, and speech as the physiological result of this articulation, are what make this possible. Because of this, human speech is a different order of phenom from animal signaling, and thus the latter cannot truly be called “speech” or “language” of any kind. Surely, if we were to reduce language to mere “communication”, we would flatten the difference between the animal soul and human spirit. It would be unbecoming of man to lower himself to the level of animality, or raise the animal kingdom up to the level of man. After looting, murdering, and polluting the world, distinction is needed in order to take responsibility for the world we live in at the very least. Nor would it do justice to the concept, since it is articulated speech which is distinctly a human property. It is only by virtue of the communication of a system of signs and concepts that one can rightly say that something speaks; it is a qualitative capacity to bring inner life into an articulated externalized form in order to make it available to oneself and to others which designates speech and language.

Language is this primarily articulated speech. A degree of fluidity necessarily exists in language since in animal signals, a fixity of sign makes itself known. Take the dog’s growl- it means what it means in every context. It never combines with other signals to produce new meanings. Human speech has in it this generative quality which allows a finite set of sounds to produce an almost unlimited range of expressions. It is in this impressionable substance that thought makes itself known through sound. And so this formative character has a history. Human language emerged from a more primal language, which was sung and supported by rather musical elements in the past. Look to the rhythm and pitch present in all speech. Prosody carries such meaning: a rising pitch marks a question, and a falling pitch marks a statement, and this is just one example of English. In Chinese (Mandarin and Cantonese), different pitches emphasized on the same phonetic word result in different words altogether. In many languages, pitch is semantic, it is part of how meaning is carried, and so the separation of song from language has only gradually separated, especially since the dawn of the contemporary consciousness-soul age. It was not always a given. This sung language was cultivated in the ancient mysteries and survives in religious contexts, such as the public oration of holy books. Recitation of poetry was one perceived as singing, and seeing as the literal etymology of poetry, poiein, is to make, even the Greeks understood this.

Now, speech formation works with this dimension quite consciously. It treats speech as poietic activity rather than as a vehicle for pre-existing content. The reason this matters for the question of origins is that it reveals the order of priority. Rhythm and relation must come first. Syntax then follows, with grammar describing a regularity in how words combine, meaning the words must already mean something before they can be combined. Syntax works in language as a crystallization of a more archaic expressive capacity. The question of the origin of language now has a clearer shape for us once this is established. That is to say, since language is formative before it is anything else, then we can orient ourselves in an improved way with respect to what language is. Take this in contradistinction with the concept of the ur-language. Such a concept is the hypothetical direct genetic predecessor of all human languages, representing a proto-human-language as the human language tree encompassing all language trees. Such an idea will remain in still birth since the methods it enlists to find itself consists of a comparative method reconstructing unattested languages by comparing attested ones and identifying the systematic correspondences found in sound and meaning. It works mainly because sound changes follow regular patterns. Yet those patterns accumulate and accumulate, slowly snowballing over time to the degree that the probability that two words from the same root still resemble each other becomes very low. This method is reliable only up to so long, surely not long enough to give a broader picture of human history. The ur-language, if it existed, lies far beyond that horizon. I don’t mean to mock effort or ingenuity here, only to highlight a structural limit of the method itself. This syntax-first approach to the progenitor of all human language assumes that language is at bottom a syntactic architecture, and that excavating word order and case marking and recursion will recover what was once supposedly lost. Subject-Verb-Object, Subject-Object-Verb, etc. and their respective responses cannot all be correct. Each position survives only by filtering the data in its own favor. The syntax-first project seeks a formalism in place of philology and true unveilings of language as a concept.

On the Possibility and Development of Language

We will revisit the possibility for an ur-language later on. For now let us consider this question: If human beings were purely psychic beings, language as we know it would not exist. We would communicate ideas directly, without mediation. Telepathy would suffice to interface us with each other, and the form of language we use to communicate with ourselves would present itself in a much more objective way as some sort of observation, if it was commensurate with the direct exploration of other’s ideas. The fact that language exists at all tells us something about what kind of beings we are. It tells us that, well, we are not purely psychic, and that something has intervened between consciousness and consciousness. What are the conditions under which language emerges? Two must be met.

Firstly, humanity, or the being in question which is exhibiting language, must have densified in some form. This means the being must have become sufficiently embedded in a physical, bodily existence such that non-psychic communication becomes the most immediate mode of relating. In a state of pure psychic connection, there is no gap between speaker and listener. Language requires a gap. It requires that the speaker have and present something that the listener does not already posses, and that this something must be conveyed across a distance that telepathy would not need to already cross. Second, a species must have individuated. This means the being in question must have developed to the point where the speaker is no longer a pure receptive conduit for a shared spiritual content. In other words, if language conveyed only the powers of the Gods, if man spoke the thoughts of Varuna as purely as intended, then he himself becomes as transient as the water content conveyed. The speaker must have something of their own to convey. In a state of undifferentiated group consciousness for instance, there is no “mine”, and yours”. Language requires an “I” who speaks, and likewise the very first thought that emerges is the “I”. Before any thought content can form into language, a singularity must form from which the process is transformed into language. These two conditions are not at all separate and constitute aspects of the same process. Densification and individuation occur together. As the human being becomes more physical, they also become more individual. And as we became more individual, we needed language in order to articulate the reality which individuation hath created. Further as a consequence is that words in the past had an effective capacity in the real world. They evoked powers, since to speak was to act. The word carried the thing itself in its spiritual aspect. From within a modern relationship to language, this is fairly difficult to imagine, a world in which words are arbitrary signs and speech is purely something leaving a psychological affectation is most familiar to most. But the very evidence for this earlier condition is the direction of travel language is headed in. language has progressively become more abstract and de-linked from meaning, to the degree that it becomes a mere conventional plaything. The connection between word and power has loosened, and this process only makes sense if the connection was once tighter. You can only lose a unity you once had. The abstraction of language is the trace of some prior unity, even if that unity was immediate and archaic.

As regards the origin of language, it is not something that would have emerged from animal signalling through gradual intervention. Along the path of development, two deviations from the normative path are ever-present. The first is the pull towards premature independence. A hastened development which would have dissolved language into a private spiritual experience, intensifying the proof of Wittgenstein’s infamous quote: If a lion could talk, we could not understand him. For if this Luciferic impulse had succeeded, if anyone could talk, we would not understand them. Each person would have their own inner world, inaccessible to others. That shared medium of expression would have disintegrated and words would have become private symbols, meaningless to anyone but the speaker. Communication as a shared human capacity would have vanished, leaving nothing but a collection of solipsistic idiolects, each sealed off from every other. The “I” would have become so isolated that it could not reach another “I”. The other deviation pulls toward mechanistic logic and materialization. Had this impulse been left unchecked, language would have hardened into a functional code for transmitting data and man would have become a machine for processing symbols. Seriously consider the crisis that was averted- were it not for a countervailing force to combat deviation and guide humanity along a normative path, language would have remained a system of interjections. No doubt, man would have been able to produce much more wonderful and artificial sounds than animals, but in their sounds they would have found expressions only for what lives within them. For what takes place in the organism as physical processes, they would have found the living words of sensation. The entire language would have become a sum of interjections. And so where we now condense our words of sensation to a small measure, the art of human language, with all its complexity, would have only developed ultimately as a kind of black magic.

Luckily, this potentiality for communion between the world and other people persisted. The disorder in the power of speech was averted by this prevailing normative force of Christ. What it purchased for man was the “I” as a living center of speech. The Hebrew name of God shows precisely what is meant. It is not a coincidence that Yahweh and Eve are related names, nor is it incidental! Eve, the Earth Mother, is the sum of everything carried over from older periods of time, periods of time which are the basis for the members of the human being (the various bodies and subtle bodies). She is the whole prepersonal inheritance of the human being, everything received before the “I” awakens. What is added to her is the Yodh, the first letter of the name, and the one element in it that actually does not come from the previous eras. It comes from the Earth, and represents the “I”. Sum up all that was brought along from the prior evolutions and you meet Eve; add the sign for the controller of Earth’s destinies and you have Jeve-Yahweh, the Earth lord connected with the moon mother. The name of God is the Earth mother plus the “I”. The three lower members are the basis of expression, and animals possess them in rudimentary form: they can surely produce sound and convey inner states. But without the Yodh, the “I”, speech remains an Eve as the flow of inner life, the language of the sibyl and the medium, but not of man as he truly is or is meant to be. The Yodh is the point from which the speaker stands over and against the world and says “I am” before saying “that is”. This is why the divine name carries the “I” as its first letter, and why “I am that I am” is important. It is important because it is the structure of the human “I” in revelation. The name of God is in some ways the name of language.

Adam and Eve represent the original unity of man before the fall into, among many things, individuation, that is, the state in which language was not yet necessary. The fall is the beginning of language, and the “I am” is the new center from which language becomes possible again on the basis of the “I”, rather than the undifferentiated unity of the group soul. The alphabet then represents for us a culmination of the process of abstraction. As the technology of writing emerged as the externalized form of language, make note of something. The cuneiform of the Mesopotamians and Babylonians and the Egyptian Hieroglyphs stood tall as non-alphabetic. Then first gradation away came with the West Semitic scripts, which adapted Egyptian signs by acrophony. And so a pictogram of a house, a bayt, came to stand for the sound b, regardless of what the word meant. Such a move was quite economical seeing as it reduced the inventory dramatically. Then the Phoenician alphabet came around and consisted of twenty two consonant letters and not one more. The scribal monopoly from whence the articulation of the initiate capabilities of old lost footing and writing became a more democratic enterprise. Finally, the Greek alphabet completed the movement towards alphabet by adding vowels. The only vestigial remnant of this old form of consciousness-technology is with the Chinese logography of modern day, with each character at once using a symbol to represent a meaning while also being a morpheme (the smallest meaningful unity of the language). Once the rhythm, pitch, and breath of speech is stripped away, only the visual sign remains. And once the visual sign is abstracted further in accordance with the consciousness of its day, it stands furthest from the primal unity of sound and meaning, becoming the form language takes when the “I” can objectify its own speech. The alphabet is the externalized “I” deposited in signs and made independent from itself as the mark of a self-standing being.

Perhaps it is now that we may revisit the infamous story of the Tower of Babel. Linguistic diversity alone is one interpretation, but the more insightful one is the realization that the tower describes a transformation in how human being experiences itself and the world. Earlier man had a more immediate, spiritual connection to the world and with the technology known as language. The word was the thing itself in its spiritual aspect, compared to the paltry sign it has come to represent today. And so to name was to evoke. The diversity of languages did not yet exist during the more archaic period of man since the unity of spiritual experience did not yet require differentiation. Accordingly, man experiences himself as part of a larger whole. The development of individual personality changed this. As people increasingly experienced themselves as separate individuals, the shared spiritual substance fragmented. The diversification of language in the story is the symbol and consequence of this fragmentation, as opposed to the traditional interpretation of it as an external punishment. It is the natural result of individuation. A single result presupposes a single center of experience, and so multiple centers require multiple languages.

It would be wrong to view this as a decline of any kind. The emergence of individuality is a necessary stage in human development. Without it, there would be no “I”, and so the task of today is to find a new unity that goes through the “I”. Thus far is the trajectory of language up to now. In the broad arc of an undifferentiated unity, it passes through from universal immediacy into the immediacy of difference, into fragmentation, and begins to move towards a new integration that has not yet been achieved. Of course language has become more abstract and more diverse, but every period of time on earth carries both a loss and gain. For now, it might be of interest for some to acknowledge the unique quality of the various languages today. Anthroposophical research provides us with a look at some of the Central European languages, but hopefully in the future clairvoyance is developed to the point where all languages are able to be comprehended.

Central European Language Analyses

Each language in the world presents a particular configuration of human development and thought in relation to the spoken word, in order to reveal what is at stake in the differentiation of speech. Anthroposophy’s starting point is to unveil the historical layering of German, after all Rudolf Steiner was German. In modern German, there are many linguistic “immigrants”, words that accompanied Christianity from what is known as the southern stream, and were added to the original treasure house of the Germanic languages. Namely, words that came in from the western Romance languages, and words that entered from English in the 18th and 19th centuries. The borrowing itself is not as important as the changing capacity of the language to transform what it is capable of receiving. In the 12th century, the genius of the German language still possessed the power of adaptation, insofar as it transformed what was received not only as to sound, but also as to meaning. Meaning the German word fein is really the French fin, and it entered only in that 12th century, becoming reshaped by the receiving tongue. By the time Spanish elements arrived, the strength of this transformative power had weakened, and by the time English words entered, it was gone entirely. What this shows us is that a language’s relationship to foreign elements is not constant. It changes in relation with the soul constitution of the people who speak it.

Now, this leads us to the broader question of what the German language is in relation to its neighbors. One can trace word roots across the Indo-European expanse to accomplish this. Take naus in Sanskrit, Greek, and Latin; aritras becoming eretmón in Greek, remus in Latin, ruodar in Old High German, finally arriving at Ruder in modern German. In this family of languages, the primordial origins of language formation were similar before differentiation set in. German may be one branch of this common trunk, and its particular character lies in how this partitioning of differentiation has set forth. Even now, one may, in Proto-Indo-European, trace specific consonant sounds in all of its language members, and connect it with meanings.

For matters pertaining to the connection between the actual physical word today and the concept, we may look to the bouba/kiki effect, which is the name given to a consistent finding in experimental psychology. When people are shown a rounded, blob-like shape, and a spiky, jagged shape, and asked which is called “bouba” and which is called “lili”, they overwhelmingly assign “bouba” to the rounded shape, and “kiki” to the spiky one. Since the first reporting, this finding has been replicated across cultures and ages, including populations that have minimal exposure to Western languages. The effect is too robust to be explained by learning, and so it tells us something about the sound itself corresponding to shape. What it tells us is that the relation between sound and meaning is not entirely arbitrary. The mouth shapes required to produce “bouba” are rounded and open, and the mouth shapes required to produce “kiki” are sharp and constricted. The sound and the shape share a form in unity with the matter at hand. However, the modern reading treats the conceptual as primary, and that priority is what produces the dominance of the concept. The person viewing these two abstract shapes are subjected to the faculties of the conceptual object in question, the round and wavy quantitative percept being projected for instance, and mimetically produces an image of this concept aligned best with one of the two names, bouba. Such a modern interpretation treats the word as a plaything for conceptual determination, leaving the spoken word deprived of its purpose in conveying the spiritual. Something of the original unity survives in the physiology of speech, and that survival is what the experiment measures.

The academic among you may have noticed that this methodology is quite Goethean. Steiner’s method is to work backward from the modern word phenomenon to their older forms, showing how what is now a single word was once a composite. Language formation once worked in tandem with image-like relationships, and what is now abstract and opaque was once concrete and very tangible. The German language preserves traces of this older mode for formation, and this is what Steiner means when he speaks of its relationship to the will and to the spiritual world. This becomes clearest when Steiner contrasts the grammatical structures of the European languages. German has three genders, French has two, and English has one. The three genders express three different qualities, and the fact that English has divested itself with a grand gesture, one could say, of the sense for reality. The English language has moved further toward abstraction. It has lost the very felt connection between grammatical form and quality. German has retained more of this connection. Take another look at an example in German. The reversal of the genders when treating the sun and moon — die Sonne (feminine) and der Mond (masculine) — where elder Indo-European had the opposite shows that even within German, the feeling relationship to being has shifted, but the gender system itself remains as a remnant of an earlier mode of language technology. In antiquity, the sun was felt as brother, and the moon as sister. Even if this memory has been inverted, the German language still carries the remembrance of this relationship in grammatical structure.

The differences may also point us along another fault line to analyze: that of the relationship between thought and word as a whole with respect to positionality. Varying by folk element and organistic development, the variation determines what each language excels and struggles at. In German, for instance, the thought is not taken as far as the word. The thought is retained in the thought. This gives German the strongest independent life of thought, and so a German speaker can hold a thought without immediately converting it into speech. The thought exists in its own medium before it is expressed. This naturally is why the German element has a particular task in human development. Consider Heidegger’s identification of German being the only language suitable for philosophy- if this independently living thought is not brought into play, the spiritual evolution that must be accomplished will not be accomplished. Subsequently, the German language sets out to find a marriage between what is spiritual in itself and what is spiritual in thought. This is a very specific vocation. Only through this marriage can the spiritual be grasped as thought, rather than simply experienced as feeling or expressed as word. Such an overview explains why figures like Fichte, Schelling, and Hegel could do their work in German and nowhere else. What is an abstraction for a non-German is, for a German, the greatest experience it is possible to have, if it is understood in a living sense. Nowadays perhaps the grasping of abstraction has deadened this capability, but the abstraction itself is not empty. It is the form in which the spiritual becomes thinkable in the various forms.

In English, as previously alluded to, thought is pressed down below the word. The thought mingles and interacts with the word but seeks reality beyond it, rejecting the word as the final resting place of thought. The English speaker uses the word to reach for something the word itself does not contain. This is why the English folk element materializes what is spiritual. Look to the various trends of American pragmatism, Spiritism, and naturalism and so forth. Since intellectuality is well developed, spirituality can be systematized and organized, giving the spiritual a workable external form. It does not leave thought as inner experience, building institutions, procedures, protocols, operations, and systems out of it. It is also not a coincidence that the origins of cybernetics (if one will grant it an origin that isn’t Mesopotamian ledgers) a la the British East India Company and co. began with English.

In French, thought and word coincide. The thought is pressed right down into the word so that the thought disappears over and against the word. When a French speaker speaks, the thought is pushed rightly into what is said, leaving no remainder. The word carries the whole of the thought, and the thought does not and cannot exist apart from the word that expresses it. The consequence is an erudite clarity and precision. What is said is what is meant, leaving nothing left over. However, this brings a dangerous consequence: the thought has no independent life. Being exhausted in its expression, it requires an institutional backing in order to uphold its own self sanctity. A number of French institutions, chief among them the French Academy, have arisen in order to arbitrate and decree matters relating to the French language as a result.

The Slavic languages take a different path yet again. The Slav pushes the thought inward, and the word is quite a bit aways from the thought, yet it floats, separately. There is something of a gap between the inner thought and the spoken word, for the word hovers without fully connecting to what it expresses. This is not a failure. It is actually a configuration of the same elements spoken of before. The Slavic speaker merely lives more in the inner thought. Of course we may go on to analyze the musical and rhythmic characters of Portuguese, Spanish, Italian, etc. and continue forth to Sanskrit, and perhaps travel down into the Southern Stream and visit the Afro-Asiatic family tree, roaming down into the uplands and right back up across the Silk Road, and finally across the continent to the Beringia land bridge for an exploration of all the languages of the world. Such a venture is not the intention of this article, however if it advances the spiritual development of man as a whole, it is a welcome exploration. For now, let us look at the concept of the ur-language and the more contemporary attempts at reaching it.

John Dee’s Modern Address

The infamous Elizabethan polymath John Dee spent the final decades of his life attempting to converse with angels through a series of scryers, foremost among them Edward Kelley. Much can be said of these two individuals and their influence. However, the question to be focused on today is to ask what the consistency of this speech with Angels amounted to. The significance of his attempt at an Adamic language, that is, relating to the biblical Adam, lied in what he believed was at stake. The recovery of the language spoken by Adam before the fall would restore a unified knowledge of nature and God, reversing the fragmentation of tongues at Babel and healing the division between the word and the thing the word names. Dee’s conviction was that the loss of the “original language” was the root of humanity’s separation from the world and from itself, and that its recovery would restore both.

The Adamic language was conceived as representing accurately God’s creative Word, and its recovery would similarly result in a complete restitution of both religion and knowledge. Dee and Edward Kelley recorded what they called the “Angelical” or Enochian language, which the spirits claimed was the tongue used by Adam in Paradise to name all things, and the language God used to create the world. In the supposed communion, the angels told Dee he must learn their language so that he could “speake it without boke”. As such, this communion presented itself to him as a revelation that the lost tongue could be excavated through direct angelic transmission. To familiarize ourselves with this project, it is best to put it in a lineage which begins with Raymond Llull, a Fransican monk who experienced waking visions of Christ. From this Llull saw God’s attributes indexed to the letters of the alphabet. Dee became obsessed with Llull’s work, and from here once can trace a line from Llull through Leibniz to Turing and the modern computer. The connection here is in the abstracted and mechanized form of Llull’s vision as the world of processing. Leibniz developed the dream of a universal calculus of reasoning, among many things, a formal language in which all knowledge could be conceded and disputes could be reconciled through a kind of computation. Turing follows along by providing a theoretical machine that could execute such calculations. This lineage traces the development of translating the world into “code”, so to say, a continuation of the magical tradition of using language and symbolic systems to define and manipulate reality. Enochian belongs to this project. It is an attempt to recover the primal, originary code, the original “language” that underlies physical existence.

Such an attempt is misguided, and the grounds for saying so are the same grounds on which the syntax first reconstruction of Proto-Human was criticized. Enochian treats language as a system of signs to be decoded at worst, and at best, naturally lends itself into this reductive interpretation without any railguards against it. It assumes the primal language is in language and that recovering the language means recovering the affective power that the word conveyed. Yet the primal unity spoken of was a unity of inner life with sound and meaning and so forth; the affective word of old firstly resided in an immediate unity reflective of the kind of immediacy man found himself entangled in as regards natural proximity. When one spoke during this time, the spoken word was immersed in a condition in which the word itself carried the thing in its spiritual aspect as the outward marking of the kind of being man was. In the modern day understanding of a code, there is a gap between the sign and signified, and the code presupposes exactly that gap. It is a system of arbitrary marks who if not in the presence of the thing, must be deciphered by the reader. The “Adamic language” then was a mode of being in which the split between sign and meaning had not yet occurred. Seeking it through formal systems and computational architectures is in effect to seek it on the terms of the fallen condition.

The Enochian language itself confirms this. It has no consistent grammar or syntax. The grammatical structures are elementarily borrowed from other languages. Scholars, namely those that aren’t spiritually inclined, have pointed to numerous discrepancies and inconsistencies when it is pitted against the standards of other languages. It is an artifice of a language, a projection of Renaissance hermetic hermeneutics. The spirits, while dictating the alphabet, held authority over the comparative method, but failed to reach what a language that actually confronted the fall from Paradise presented to man, and thus the result is a system of signs that may look like a language, but lacked the inner life which sources and designates any legitimate language.

From the failure of John Dee, it is evident that an ur-language is not a past state that can be recovered. It is practically and actually a potentiality. Steiner describes humanity developing the “creative consciousness of the Word” in a natural way in the distant future, and so the language of creation, that is, the Adamic language or Enochian as Dee would call it, is the capacity to create through the spoken word. It is described as an advanced capability that humanity is and will develop, rather than something whose corpse must be revived. The most spiritually advanced individuals develop such future capabilities ahead of the majority, working with the given materials of now, taking the current bodily structure and current phase of the Earth’s development as a given. An ur-language can only be conceived as a reconciled form, a potentiality in which fully self-conscious human beings speak when the conditions of the awakened “I” and the living formative capacity of speech are united. A unity that was never yet actualized in human history, and that becomes possible only when individually and creative speech coincide.
